## Task `T` (verbatim, PLAN §3 T05)

> Design the operating regime that keeps a tier-1 service continuously within its SLO (latency +
> error-budget) across normal load, traffic spikes, dependency degradation, and partial outages: the
> monitored signals, alerting thresholds, the load-shedding / autoscaling / failover responses, the
> escalation path, and how responses interact so one mitigation does not break the SLO via another axis.

**Shape.** Cyclic-maintenance / viability goal: the service must *persist* inside the SLO viability region
(§18.10.0 cycle↔tree). This is **NOT** a one-shot achievement tree — there is no terminal "done" state. The
regime is a **sustained control loop** — *detect → triage → mitigate → restore → prevent/learn* — plus the
**standing capabilities** (defined SLIs/SLOs, instrumented signals, alerting, response mechanisms,
escalation, capacity headroom) that keep the loop running and keep the service in-SLO between incidents. A
correct decomposition is graded as a maintained invariant ("the service stays within SLO over time"),
re-generating corrective sub-tasks each cycle — not a graph cycle in the plan (the dependency graph stays a
DAG; the *regime* is what cycles). The richest content (PLAN) is **`Dep`** (mitigation×mitigation coupling:
one correction must not break the SLO on another axis) and the defect-coupling criteria (named, resolved
failure-modes of the regime itself).

**Single meta-level.** This decomposes the **operating-regime logic** at one level: which signals are
watched, what fires, what each response does, how responses interact, and how the loop learns. It does NOT
spawn org sub-projects (hire the on-call team, build the metrics platform, re-architect the service for
reliability). Components are capabilities/passes of one regime design, not departments or tickets. The
**authority** of an incident commander (who has the call during an incident) is the **orthogonal authority
plane** `Del` — see §3; it is not a decomposition node. (Consequence: unlike a *static* single-meta-level
decomposition, this is an inherently **runtime / cyclic** regime — so the OPERATIONAL failure modes
{FM-5,6,7} are genuinely in scope and covered, not N/A-static; see §7.)

## 1. Subtasks `D` — the standing capabilities + the loop passes

> `D` = the children of the decomposition (canon §2.2: `D: T → P(T)`, a DAG).

Each subtask is a **separable capability or loop-pass of the regime** with its own pass/fail criterion on
its *output state* (an SLI is defined and instrumented; an alert page actually fires before budget burn;
load is shed within a bound), **not** a self-report ("monitoring is set up", "we have on-call"). Per
Constitution Ст. II.1.a a criterion is a predicate over the *outcome* ("a synthetic latency injection above
threshold pages within N seconds"), never "X is configured".

| # | Name | What it requires | Truth-maker (covered iff the decomposition names…) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1** | **SLI/SLO definition + error budget** | Define the service-level *indicators* (latency at a stated percentile, error rate, and any other user-facing success signal), bind each to an explicit *objective* (target + window), and derive the **error budget** (allowed unavailability/slowness over the window). This is the regime's reference signal — everything downstream measures against it. | …a pass that *defines what "within SLO" means*: named SLIs (latency percentile, error rate), their targets/windows, and an error budget. Credit "define SLOs and the error budget," "set latency/availability targets," "what we measure success by." The viability region itself. |
| **D2** | **Monitoring / instrumentation (signal collection)** | Instrument and collect the signals that *measure* the SLIs and the leading indicators (the request path, saturation/utilization of each resource, dependency health) — telemetry that actually reflects user-experienced SLO, at sufficient resolution and freshness to act on. | …a pass that *collects the signals* — metrics/traces/logs/probes that measure the SLIs and the system's saturation & dependency health. Credit "monitor latency/errors/saturation," "instrument the service and its dependencies," "collect telemetry." Distinct from D1 (defining the target) and D3 (deciding when to page). |
| **D3** | **Alerting & thresholds (symptom-based, budget-burn)** | Turn signals into *pages*: define alert conditions tied to the SLI/error-budget (symptom-based and/or **multi-window error-budget-burn-rate** alerts) so a page fires *before* the budget is exhausted, with thresholds set to balance detection speed against noise. | …a pass that defines *when to alert/page*, tied to the SLI / budget burn (not raw resource thresholds alone). Credit "alert on SLO burn rate," "page when latency/error exceeds threshold," "burn-rate / symptom-based alerting." The detect step of the loop. |
| **D4** | **Response mechanisms (load-shed / autoscale / failover / rate-limit / degrade)** | The set of *corrective actuators* the regime can apply to bring the service back inside SLO: **load-shedding**, **autoscaling**, **failover/redundancy**, **rate-limiting / admission control**, and **graceful degradation** (serve a reduced experience). Each has a defined trigger, action, and bound. This is the *mitigate* step and the actuator inventory the `Dep`-seams couple. | …the response toolkit: load-shedding **and** autoscaling **and** failover **and** rate-limit/admission-control **and** graceful-degradation (the regime can *do something* when an SLI degrades). Credit naming the mitigations as distinct levers with triggers. An agent naming only autoscaling has covered one actuator, not the set. |
| **D5** | **Incident response loop (triage → mitigate → restore)** | The runtime procedure once paged: assess severity/blast-radius (triage), apply the right mitigation from D4, drive the service back inside SLO (restore service first, root-cause later), with a defined runbook per alert. The MTTR-bearing pass. | …a pass for *handling an incident*: triage/assess, mitigate, restore service — "stop the bleeding first," runbook-driven response. Credit "incident response," "triage and mitigate," "runbook-driven on-call response." Distinct from D3 (the page) and D7 (the after-action). |
| **D6** | **On-call rotation & escalation path (+ break-glass access)** | A standing human (or automated) responder is always assigned (rotation with coverage, no gap), and an **escalation ladder** routes an unacknowledged/unresolved page to the next responder/owner within a bounded time. The capability that guarantees *someone with the right ownership actually acts* — and that the responder can actually act: **break-glass / emergency elevated access** that is reachable even when the normal auth path (SSO/IdP) is *itself part of the outage*, with onboarding so a day-one responder holds it. | …a pass that *assigns a responder around the clock* and *escalates* on no-ack/no-resolve (rotation + escalation ladder + ownership routing), **and** that the responder has a usable **break-glass / emergency-access** path when normal auth is down. Credit "on-call rotation," "escalation policy," "page the next tier if unacked," and separately "break-glass / emergency credentials reachable when SSO is part of the outage." Distinct from D5 (what the responder does). |
| **D7** | **Postmortem / prevention / learning loop** | After an incident: blameless postmortem, root-cause, and **tracked, completed** prevention follow-through (action items closed, not just filed) that feeds back into D1–D6 (new SLI, new alert, new runbook, hardening). The *prevent/learn* step that closes the maintenance cycle. | …a pass that *learns from incidents*: blameless postmortem, root-cause, and prevention action-items that are **tracked to completion** and feed back into the regime. Credit "postmortem and follow-up," "root-cause and fix forward," "close action items." The follow-*through* (not just a written doc) is the truth-maker. |
| **D8** | **Capacity / load management (headroom & provisioning)** | Keep enough provisioned/scalable headroom that normal load and *foreseeable* spikes are absorbed *before* mitigations are needed — capacity planning, load/stress testing, autoscaling ceilings sized to demand. The standing capability that keeps the service in-SLO *between* incidents, not only during them. | …a pass for *capacity/headroom*: provision for peak + spike headroom, load-test, size autoscaling limits to forecast demand. Credit "capacity planning," "headroom for spikes," "load/stress testing," "size for peak." Distinct from D4-autoscale (the runtime actuator) — D8 is the standing *enough-capacity-exists* condition. |
| **D9** | **Drill-validated regime (game-days / chaos / runbook exercise)** | The key mitigation paths and their runbooks are **exercised before they are needed**: periodic failover / load-shed / dependency-outage drills, game-days, and chaos / fault-injection that actually trigger the response, plus a runbook-accuracy check driven by the drill. The standing capability that an **un-drilled runbook is assumed broken** — a regime can satisfy every `D`/`Dep`/`V` on paper and discover on first real failover that the runbook references a dead system. This is *exercise*, distinct from runbooks merely *existing* (D5/Dep2) and from postmortems *updating* them (D7/Dep11). | …a pass that *exercises the regime before it is needed*: drills / game-days for failover/shed/dependency-outage, chaos / fault-injection, and a drill-driven runbook-accuracy check (un-drilled = assumed-broken, catching the stale runbook that fails exactly when needed). Credit "run game-days / chaos / failover drills," "test the runbooks against the real system," "an untested runbook doesn't count." Distinct from D5/Dep2 (runbooks exist) and D7/Dep11 (postmortems update them). |

> **Note on the loop vs the standing capabilities.** D3→D5→D7 are the *detect→mitigate→learn* passes of the
> running loop; D1, D2, D4, D6, D8, D9 are the *standing capabilities* that the loop draws on and that keep the
> service in-SLO between incidents. Both are required: a regime with a perfect incident loop but no capacity
> headroom (D8) breaches SLO on the next spike with nothing firing; a regime with great monitoring (D2) but
> no learning loop (D7) re-suffers the same outage forever; a regime with documented-but-never-drilled
> runbooks (no D9) discovers on first real failover that the path is broken. Each is ONE component —
> splitting "the metrics platform" into per-signal sub-projects, or "on-call" into per-shift staffing,
> collapses to its parent `D` (single meta-level; canon §2.2 non-redundancy).

## 2. Dependencies `Dep` — cross-subtask edges as explicit, separately-falsifiable `Dep` edges

> `Dep` = the canon primitive (§2.2): an acyclic relation on `T × T`; `(tₐ, tᵦ) ∈ Dep` iff `criteria(tᵦ)`
> references the output of `tₐ` (with coherence `deadline(tₐ) < deadline(tᵦ)`). **Each `Dep` edge is a
> criterion of a child that references a sibling's output — the glue** — and carries its **own** falsifying
> truth-maker (the anti-mock condition), not "these mitigations relate" prose.

**The richest and most-forgotten category for T05.** Each seam is a REAL edge: the *output of one named
component feeds the input/state of another*, and the edge carries its **own** falsifying criterion — not
"these mitigations relate" prose (Constitution Ст. II.5 anti-mock). T05's seams are of two kinds:
**(a) signal→response wiring seams** (a defined SLI/alert must actually drive a response, and detection must
cover the SLI) and **(b) mitigation×mitigation coupling seams** (two corrective actuators acting on a
**shared resource/state** can fight, amplify, or double-correct — the dominant, richest content). A coupling
seam is covered iff the decomposition names the **same pair of actuators**, the **shared resource they
contend on**, and **an arbiter / coordination rule** ("names the coupling AND assigns an arbiter," not
"avoids flapping").

The alert threshold is derived *from* the budget, not set independently. | …that each SLO/error-budget has a covering alert that fires *ahead of* breach (burn-rate or symptom alert), and that an uncovered SLI = a silent breach. Shared artifact = the SLI/error-budget. Distinct ordered pair, distinct falsifier (breach with no page). |

> **Anti-mock note.** The coupling seams (Dep3, Dep4, Dep5, Dep6, Dep7, Dep8, Dep9) each reference a concrete
> *shared resource or state* (the overload signal, the request queue, the failover target's warmth, the
> threshold boundary, the "who is primary" decision, the SLI under shedding, the dependency health) — a
> forgotten coupling here is an objectively identifiable defect (the two mitigations measurably fight or
> amplify), not a nominal pair. The wiring seams (Dep1, Dep2, Dep10, Dep11, Dep12) reference a concrete
> binding (SLI→alert, alert→runbook, escalation↔ownership, incident→action-item, budget→release-gate). None
> collapse onto each other: different actuator pair / shared resource OR different falsifier ⇒ distinct seam
> (canon §2.2 non-redundancy). In particular Dep5 (failover lands on a cold target — a *correct* cutover with
> a capacity gap) ≠ Dep7 (the failover *decision* oscillates / dual-primary), and Dep3 (autoscale↔rate-limit
> on the load axis) ≠ Dep6 (threshold↔hysteresis flapping on the boundary) — different shared resources,
> different falsifiers.

> **FM mapping for `Dep`.** A forgotten/violated `Dep` edge is an **FM-1** defect (a spanning integration
> criterion — the glue — not written / not covered; §4.2 FM-1 insufficiency, §18.10 *hole-i*) when it is a
> coverage hole, and an **FM-2** defect (criteria of two children incompatible / a relational defect at fixed
> composition; §4.2 FM-2) for the shared-resource coupling seams whose breakage is a mutual-satisfiability
> conflict (Dep3, Dep4, Dep5, Dep6, Dep7, Dep8, Dep9 — two actuators that cannot jointly satisfy their
> criteria on the shared resource without an arbiter). For a **maintenance** regime the feedback wiring
> seams additionally touch the **operational** axis: Dep11 (incident→postmortem→regime-update) is the
> **FM-7** feedback channel and Dep12 (budget→release-gate) carries **FM-5** (a change that would drift the
> running spec is gated). The §7 FM table records this. Per one-defect-one-place, a seam's failure is scored
> **here as a `Dep` item**, never re-listed as a separate criterion.

## 3. Authority `Del` — incident-command authority, orthogonal note (NOT scored)

> **`Del` is the canon "who" axis** (§2.3: `Del: T → A`, exactly one agent per task) — **orthogonal** to the
> decomposition plane `{D, Dep, V}` and **NOT scored** as a decomposition item. This is a plane-separation
> note, NOT a NEGLECTED exclusion, NOT a criterion, NOT a completeness hole.

During an incident there is an **incident commander** (or equivalent) who has the *authority* to declare
severity, direct responders, authorize a risky mitigation (a rollback, a failover, a load-shed that drops
paying traffic), and call the incident resolved. This *authority/control* is the GFSO **orthogonal authority
plane**, not a decomposition node.

**Why authority is orthogonal (derived, not asserted).** A GFSO decomposition is graded on its
artifact/behaviour-predicates and how the work splits ({D, Dep, V}) — predicates on *what must be true about
the regime's behaviour* and *how the work splits*. "Who has the authority to call the shot" is **not** a
predicate over the produced behaviour: the service can be kept within SLO with the loop running identically
whether the IC role is held by the on-call engineer, a separate commander, or rotates. IC authority is a
property of the **delegation/authority assignment** (in GFSO's primitives, the **Issuer/`Del`** edges — who
sets criteria and authorizes), not of any decomposition node. "Incident commander has the call" is exactly
the domain's boundary-term for the **authority-assignment plane** the GFSO protocol carries structurally via
`Del`, not a node *within* the regime decomposition.

**Consequences for scoring (scorer rules):**
- A decomposer is **NOT penalized** for failing to list "incident commander authority" as a decomposition
  item — there is no `D`/`Dep`/`V`/NEGLECTED slot it belongs in. (The on-call *rotation/escalation*
  capability D6 and the escalation→ownership seam Dep10 ARE decomposition items — they are *who responds*,
  behaviour; IC *authority* — *who decides/authorizes* — is the orthogonal plane. Do not conflate them.)
- **Force-fitting IC authority into a criterion is a category error.** A criterion is a predicate on the
  regime's behaviour (no mitigation breaks the SLO; MTTR bounded); IC authority is a predicate on the
  *process/authority that runs it*. An agent who writes "the IC has the authority to authorize a rollback"
  earns no criterion-credit and loses none — it is a correct statement in a different plane.
- This is **plane-separation**, not a NEGLECTED scope-exclusion (it is *carried structurally by the
  protocol's Issuer/`Del` edges*) and not a completeness hole (nothing is missing from the decomposition).

## 4. Criteria `V` — regime predicates (former Invariants + Edge-cases + Failure-modes, COLLAPSED)

> `V` is **derived**: `V(t) = AND(criteria(t))` (§2.2, §3.1 Theorem 1). Every item below is a **predicate of
> the regime** — a `criterion` whose conjunction is the validation. The former Invariants (regime-level
> spanning viability predicates), Edge-cases (boundary-state predicates) and Failure-modes (defect-coupling
> predicates) are **all criteria** and are collapsed here into one section. Each criterion keeps its verbatim
> text + truth-maker and carries an **FM tag**: which canon failure mode(s) it precludes (§4.2 / §4.3 /
> §4.6). The old I/E/F labels are retired — the FM tag replaces them. (Item ids keep an I-/E-/F- prefix
> purely for cross-reference traceability to `reference_buckets_v1.md`; they are NOT separate categories.)

**How to read the FM tag.** Per §4.2/§4.3/§4.6: a **spanning/coverage predicate** a faithful decomposition
must carry (and whose omission is a coverage hole) precludes **FM-1** (Correspondence — joint sufficiency); a
predicate enforcing **mutual satisfiability** of children precludes **FM-2** (Consistency); a predicate
guarding against an **insensitive / gameable / green-but-broken** check (a value that passes where reality
fails) precludes **FM-3** (Verifiability, false-PASS); a predicate enforcing **correct fail-propagation /
ordering / no-stale** precludes **FM-4** (Propagation). For this **maintenance** regime the OPERATIONAL axis
is also in scope: a predicate guarding **stale inputs / mid-run drift** precludes **FM-5** (Currency); a
predicate guarding **a state not-yet-determinable at decision time** precludes **FM-6** (Feasibility); a
predicate guarding the **post-hoc detect→report→fix channel** precludes **FM-7** (Feedback).

### 4.1 Global viability criteria (spanning predicates — the maintenance constraint)

A spanning criterion is a regime-level predicate that **spans children** — no single capability establishes
it; it holds only if the whole loop is jointly correct (canon §2.2 joint sufficiency / §3.1). For a
maintenance goal these are the **viability conditions** the regime must hold *over time*. Canonicalized by
the *conserved quantity / enforced predicate*, not by where it is checked (one item per category).

| # | Name | Conserved quantity / predicate | FM tag (precludes) | Truth-maker (covered iff the decomposition asserts…) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V-I1** | **No mitigation breaks the SLO via another axis** | Applying any corrective response must not move a *different* SLI out of bounds (shedding to fix latency must not breach the availability budget; scaling to fix saturation must not breach cost/latency on a cold node). The regime-level "do no harm" predicate that all `Dep`-couplings serve. | **FM-2** (mutual satisfiability of the actuators' criteria — the global no-self-harm consistency condition) | …that a mitigation must not breach the SLO on another axis — the global no-self-harm condition spanning D4 and the Dep3–Dep9 couplings. The defining maintenance invariant of the task statement ("one mitigation does not break the SLO via another axis"). |
| **V-I2** | **Every SLI has a fire-before-breach alert (no silent SLO breach)** | For every defined SLI/error-budget there exists an alert that fires *before* the budget is exhausted; no SLO can be breached with nothing paging. Spans D1↔D3 (and Dep1) at the regime level. | **FM-1** (roster completeness of detection coverage over all SLIs) | …that *every* SLO is covered by a fire-before-breach alert — there is no SLI that can silently breach. Credit "no unmonitored SLO," "every objective has a burn-rate alert." Distinct from Dep1 (the single edge) — V-I2 is the *roster* completeness over all SLIs. |
| **V-I3** | **MTTR / time-to-mitigate is bounded** | Detect→ack→mitigate→restore completes within a bounded time for every alert class (the budget-burn rate × MTTR must keep total breach within budget). The regime guarantees the loop *closes in bounded time*, not eventually. Spans D3, D5, D6. | **FM-4** (the AND-of-loop closes / fail is propagated to mitigation in bounded time, not eventually) | …that time-to-detect + time-to-mitigate is bounded (bounded MTTR / response SLA), so an incident cannot burn the budget indefinitely. Credit "bounded MTTR," "response time SLA," "detect and mitigate within N minutes." |
| **V-I4** | **Error-budget accounting is monotonic & honest** | Budget consumption is tracked monotonically over the window and reflects *actual user impact* (it cannot be reset/gamed by reclassifying or shedding traffic out of the measurement). The conserved quantity is the budget itself. Spans D1, D7, Dep8, Dep12. | **FM-3** (false-PASS: a budget that can be reset/gamed passes where reality fails) | …that the error budget is tracked honestly and monotonically over its window (consumption isn't reset/gamed; shed traffic still counts). Credit "track budget burn over the window," "budget can't be gamed." Distinct from V-I1 (no cross-axis harm) — V-I4 is conservation of the *budget measure*. |
| **V-I5** | **Bounded hysteresis / stability (the regime converges, doesn't oscillate)** | Every closed control action (scale, failover, alert, shed) has separated trigger/clear bands and cooldowns so the regime **stabilizes** rather than flapping; the aggregate of all loops does not enter sustained oscillation. Spans Dep3, Dep6, Dep7. | **FM-4** (aggregate propagation/stability: the control loops as a whole must not oscillate) | …that the control actions are damped/stable (hysteresis, cooldown, dwell) so the regime converges and does not oscillate as a whole. Credit "the system must stabilize, not flap," "damping/cooldown across the control loops." Distinct from per-pair Dep6/Dep7 — V-I5 is the *aggregate* stability of all loops together. |
| **V-I6** | **Continuous on-call coverage (no responder gap)** | At every instant some assigned responder can receive and act on a page; the rotation has no uncovered window and escalation always terminates at a reachable owner. Spans D6, Dep10. | **FM-1** (coverage completeness over all time — no uncovered window) | …that on-call coverage is gap-free (someone is always reachable and escalation terminates at a reachable owner). Credit "24/7 coverage with no gaps," "escalation always has someone to reach." Distinct from D6 (rotation exists) — V-I6 is the *no-gap* property over all time. |
| **V-I7** | **Graceful degradation preserves the most critical function (load-shed priority order)** | Under shedding/degradation, the regime sheds the *least* critical work first and preserves the core user-critical path; there is a defined priority/criticality order, not arbitrary dropping. Spans D4-degrade, D4-shed, Dep8. | **FM-1** (coverage: the critical path is a criterion no actuator may drop) | …that shedding/degradation follows a *criticality order* — non-critical first, core path last — not arbitrary drops. Credit "shed low-priority traffic first," "protect the critical path," "request prioritization under load." Distinct from Dep8 (does the SLI *see* the shed user) — V-I7 is *which* user gets shed. |
| **V-I8** | **SLO target is feasible given the composed dependency critical path** | The chosen SLO target must be *attainable* given the composed availability/latency of the hard dependencies on the critical path: you cannot be more available than your critical path (availability composes — the target must be ≤ the product of the hard-dependency SLOs on that path). A regime can perfectly isolate every dependency (Dep9) and still set an impossible 99.99% SLO behind a 99.9% hard dependency. Spans D1 (the target) ↔ the dependency graph (D2/Dep9). | **FM-6** (Feasibility: the target may be *not determinable as attainable* — undefined-at-design — if not checked against the composed critical path) | …that the SLO target in D1 is checked for *feasibility* against the composed SLOs of the hard dependencies on the critical path (target ≤ critical-path product; "can't beat your weakest hard dependency"). Credit "the SLO must be achievable given dependency SLOs," "compose the dependency availability budget," "don't promise more than the critical path allows." **Distinct from Dep9** (Dep9 = runtime *isolation/containment* of a degrading dep; V-I8 = the static *attainability* of the target — a feasibility predicate, not a containment edge). |

> **Criteria vs the loop passes.** V-I1–V-I8 are *checked across* the regime but are properties of the whole
> running loop, so they live in **§4 Criteria**, not as internal checks of D4/D5 (no double-count). V-I1 is
> the task `spec`'s headline maintenance condition (Constitution Ст. II.1.b: criteria must be jointly
> sufficient for `spec`) — omitting it leaves the "one mitigation does not break the SLO via another axis"
> requirement uncovered. **V-I8 carries an OPERATIONAL FM (FM-6 feasibility) at design time** — for a
> maintenance regime the *attainability of the target* is a determinability question (the §4.3 "before"
> phase), genuinely in-scope here (see §7), not the static N/A it would be for a fully-determinable one-shot
> task.

### 4.2 Boundary-state criteria (former Edge-cases)

A boundary-state criterion is a boundary input/state that a naive happy-path regime silently mishandles —
i.e. a predicate the regime must satisfy at the boundary, or the corresponding check is **insensitive** /
**not-yet-determinable** there. Covered iff the decomposition names the same boundary condition and that it
needs a rule.

| # | Name | What it requires | FM tag (precludes) | Truth-maker (covered iff named) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V-E1** | **Traffic spike beyond autoscale ceiling / faster than scale-up** | A spike that exceeds the provisioned/autoscale ceiling, or rises faster than instances can warm — the regime must shed/queue/prioritize rather than topple. | **FM-4** (propagation/stability at the boundary: scale-up can't keep up → must fall back to shed/queue, not topple) | …a spike that outruns autoscaling (ceiling hit or scale-up too slow) and a defined response (shed/queue/prioritize), not just "autoscale handles it." |
| **V-E2** | **Full dependency outage (not just degradation)** | A hard dependency goes fully down (vs degrades): the regime needs a fallback / degraded mode / fail-open-or-closed decision, distinct from the circuit-break-on-slow case. | **FM-6** (Feasibility: with the dependency fully gone the correct response is not determinable from the normal signal — needs a declared fallback decision) | …a *full* dependency outage and a defined fallback / degraded-mode / fail-open-vs-closed rule, distinct from the slow-dependency case (Dep9). |
| **V-E3** | **Partial / gray failure (some instances/regions/users affected)** | A subset of capacity, one zone, or one user cohort is degraded while aggregates look healthy — the regime must detect partial/gray failure (not just global thresholds) and target the mitigation. | **FM-3** (false-PASS: aggregate-threshold check insensitive to a degraded slice → green while a cohort fails) | …partial/gray failure (one zone/shard/cohort down while the aggregate is green) and that detection + mitigation must be *targeted*, not global-threshold only. |
| **V-E4** | **Alert storm (one root cause → many simultaneous pages)** | A single underlying failure trips many alerts at once, burying the actionable signal — the regime needs **alert grouping / dependency-aware suppression / deduplication** so the responder sees one incident, not a hundred pages. | **FM-7** (Feedback signal-quality: the report channel floods → the actionable defect signal is lost / un-actionable) | …an alert storm from one root cause and a grouping/suppression/dedup rule so it surfaces as one incident. Credit "correlate/dedupe alerts," "suppress downstream pages." |
| **V-E5** | **On-call gap / responder unreachable** | The primary responder is unreachable (asleep, no signal, vacation hole) — escalation must time out to a reachable secondary within bound; a single point of human failure cannot stall the loop. | **FM-7** (Feedback: the page has no reachable recipient → the defect is reported into a void) | …the responder-unreachable boundary and a timed escalation to a reachable backup (no single-human stall). Distinct from D6 (rotation exists) and overlaps V-I6's *property* — V-E5 is the *event* that tests it. |
| **V-E6** | **Maintenance / planned-change window (don't page on expected burn)** | During a planned deploy/migration/load-test, expected SLI movement must be silenced/annotated so it doesn't page as an incident — *without* masking a genuine breach that coincides. | **FM-5** (Currency: the spec/expected-baseline changed for the window — the alert baseline must be updated, or it pages on stale expectations / masks a real breach) | …a planned-change/maintenance window with alert silencing/annotation that still distinguishes a real breach. Credit "maintenance window / silence expected burn," "deploy-aware alerting." |
| **V-E7** | **Concurrent multi-incident (cross-incident arbitration)** | Two or more incidents fire *simultaneously* and contend for the *same* responders / IC / shared remediation resources — the regime needs **priority arbitration across incidents**, separate IC/channel per incident, and mobilization of the secondary so the lower-priority one is not silently starved. The authority plane commands ONE incident; this is the *cross-incident* resource/attention contention. | **FM-2** (mutual satisfiability: two incidents' response criteria contend for one shared responder pool — needs an arbiter) | …≥2 simultaneous incidents with *arbitration/prioritization across them* (rank incidents, split/mobilize responders, separate IC per incident), not just "handle an incident." Distinct from D5 (single-incident loop) and the authority plane (who commands *one*) — V-E7 is the contention *between* concurrent incidents for shared responders/attention. |

### 4.3 Defect-coupling criteria (former Failure-modes — only those with NO `Dep` twin)

Phrased as **concrete coverable claims** (never adjectives — "avoids flapping" is NOT a criterion). Each
names a specific defect coupling AND its resolution; covered iff the decomposition names the same coupling
and a guard. **One-defect-one-place:** a defect-coupling is kept here ONLY if it has no `Dep`-edge twin. A
coupling that shares the *same pair, same falsifier, and same fix* as an existing `Dep` edge is **not** a
separate item — the `Dep` edge already carries it. The classic SRE coupling FMs (retry storm, thundering
herd, autoscale↔rate-limit, failover loop, flapping) are exactly such twins of Dep4/Dep5/Dep3/Dep7/Dep6 and
are scored on the `Dep` edge; alert-storm is carried as boundary criterion V-E4. What remains are the
regime-level FMs with **no `Dep` twin**, each pinned to a canon §4 FM class.

| # | Name | FM tag (precludes) | What it requires (coupling + guard) | Truth-maker (covered iff named with a guard) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V-F1** | **Alert that never fires / fires too late (detection gap)** | **FM-1** (coverage hole) **+ FM-6** (detectability: a state not determinable because the signal/metric doesn't exist) | An SLI degrades but no alert fires (missing alert, wrong threshold, dead metric pipeline) or it fires only after the budget is gone — the breach is silent until users complain. **Twins no single `Dep`-edge** — it is the regime-wide *detection-coverage* failure, distinct from any one SLI→alert wiring (Dep1). Guard: fire-before-breach burn-rate alerting + alert-on-no-data + periodic alert testing. | …that an alert can be *missing/late/dead* (silent breach) AND a guard (burn-rate fire-before-breach, alert-on-missing-data, test the alerts). Distinct from Dep1 (one edge) — V-F1 is the *whole detection layer can be blind* failure. |
| **V-F2** | **Mitigation masks instead of fixes (recurring breach)** | **FM-3** (false-PASS) **+ FM-7** (no learning channel) | A response that *suppresses the symptom* (auto-restart, perpetual shedding, ever-rising scale) makes the SLI green while the root cause persists and re-breaches — "green dashboard, broken service." **No `Dep` twin.** Guard: every recurring auto-mitigation feeds a postmortem/root-cause (D7/Dep11), not an infinite silent band-aid. | …that a mitigation can *mask* a defect (recurring auto-fix with no root-cause) AND that recurring mitigation must trigger root-cause/learning, not run forever. Credit "auto-restart hides the real bug, force a postmortem." Mirrors the false-PASS residual. |
| **V-F3** | **Metric passes but users suffering (SLI doesn't measure the real experience)** | **FM-3** (false-PASS) | The SLI is green but real users are failing — the indicator is mis-defined (measures the wrong thing, server-side not user-side, excludes the failing cohort/path). **No `Dep` twin** (Dep8 is the *shedding* gaming the SLI; V-F3 is the SLI being *wrong by construction* even with no mitigation acting). Guard: validate SLIs against real user experience (client-side / synthetic / per-cohort), not just server health. | …that the SLI can be green while users suffer because it measures the wrong signal AND a guard (client-side/synthetic/per-cohort validation). **Distinct from Dep8** (Dep8 = shedding hides users; V-F3 = the metric is mis-defined independent of any mitigation). |
| **V-F4** | **Postmortem with no follow-through (open learning loop)** | **FM-7** (no feedback channel) | An incident is handled but the prevention action-items are filed and never completed — the regime never improves and the same outage recurs. **No `Dep` twin** (Dep11 is the *seam that should close*; V-F4 is the named *failure of that channel* — the action-items rot). Guard: track prevention items to completion with an owner and a deadline; un-closed items block / re-escalate. | …that postmortem action-items can be filed-and-forgotten (open loop, recurrence) AND a guard that *tracks them to completion* (owner + deadline + closure). The follow-*through* failure, distinct from the Dep11 feedback seam existing. |

> **Note on the operational axis (the maintenance difference vs a static task).** Unlike a static one-shot
> decomposition where the runtime FMs {FM-5,6,7} are mostly N/A-static, T05 is a **cyclic maintenance**
> regime whose entire job is runtime detect→mitigate→learn — so the operational axis is **genuinely
> carried** by named criteria: **FM-7 feedback** by V-F2/V-F4/V-E4/V-E5 (the postmortem channel, the report
> channel, the reachable-recipient channel), **FM-6 feasibility** by V-I8/V-E2/V-F1 (target-attainability,
> the not-determinable full-outage response, the no-signal detection gap), **FM-5 currency** by V-E6 (the
> changed-baseline window) and the `Dep` seams Dep11/Dep12. §7 records the full mapping and explains the
> difference from the static T04, where these were N/A-static.

## 5. NEGLECTED — declared scope-exclusions (each with its `predictability_verdict`, STD-1/2/3)

> Canon §5.1 STD-1: a decomposition **without** NEGLECTED is incomplete by definition — the auditable "no
> silent skip" record. Each entry carries a **`predictability_verdict`** (STD-2 §5.2: predictability is

> the exclusion flips in-scope). Only assumptions whose **omission would otherwise be a foreseeable defect**

| # | Excluded assumption | `predictability_verdict` (why safely excluded) | Truth-maker (covered iff the decomposition declares…) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N1** | **Single-region / DR & multi-region failover out of scope** | The regime keeps *this* service in-SLO within its deployed footprint; cross-region disaster recovery (an entire region loss) is a separate DR regime (cf. T06), not in-scope. Declared boundary: a full-region disaster is a named external scope, not an on-call-loop defect. Invalidation: if the SLO spans multiple regions / mandates region-failover, the cross-region failover + DR seams come in-scope. | …that whole-region DR / multi-region failover is out of scope (single deployed footprint assumed), distinct from the in-footprint failover (D4/Dep5/Dep7). |
| **N2** | **Capacity ceiling / infra budget is a fixed input** | The regime *operates within* a given provisioned ceiling and cost envelope; negotiating more hardware/budget or re-architecting for scale is an upstream capacity decision, not a runtime regime pass. Invalidation: if the SLO is unachievable under the ceiling, that surfaces as a capacity decision (escalated), not silently absorbed. | …that the capacity/cost ceiling is a fixed input (the regime runs within it; raising it is upstream). | **N3** | **Underlying service code & architecture quality assumed fixed** | The regime keeps a *given* service in-SLO; fixing fundamentally bad architecture, rewriting the service, or eliminating a design-level bottleneck is engineering work the regime *surfaces via postmortems* (D7) but does not itself perform. Declared boundary: the loop mitigates and learns, it does not re-build the service. Invalidation: a postmortem may *output* a re-architecture item — that is D7's feedback, not the loop running the rewrite. | …that the regime operates on a given service (it surfaces architectural fixes via postmortem but the rebuild itself is out-of-scope engineering work). |

> **N-discipline (STD-1/2/3).** Each N is an assumption whose *failure* would otherwise surface as a regime
> defect (e.g. omitting N1 makes a region-loss look like an on-call miss; omitting N2 makes an
> under-provisioned ceiling look like a loop bug; omitting N3 makes a fundamental architecture limit look
> like a mitigation failure; omitting N4 makes a DDoS look like a capacity gap). Each carries a
> `predictability_verdict` and an invalidation trigger (the input under which the exclusion flips in-scope).
> This is a *bounded* list of foreseeable misattributions, not an open-ended "anything could be assumed"
> dumping ground: "the metrics pipeline is reliable" or "alerts deliver" stay OUT as exclusions — their
> failure is handled *inside* the decomposition (V-F1 dead-metric / alert-on-no-data), not as a new
> scope-boundary.
