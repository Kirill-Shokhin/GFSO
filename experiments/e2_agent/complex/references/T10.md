## Task `T` (verbatim, PLAN §3 T10)

> Design the control regime that keeps a commercial greenhouse continuously within the crop's viable
> envelope (temperature, humidity, CO₂, light, irrigation) across day/night and weather swings: the sensed
> variables, the actuators (heating, ventilation, shading, CO₂ injection, irrigation, supplemental light),
> and the rules coupling them so that correcting one variable does not push another out of bounds.

**Shape.** Cyclic-maintenance / viability goal: the regime must *hold* the greenhouse state inside the
crop's viability kernel **indefinitely** (§18.10.0 cycle↔tree). This is **NOT** a one-shot achievement tree
— there is no terminal "done" state. The regime is a **sustained control loop** — *sense → decide →
actuate → observe* — running each control interval, plus the **standing capabilities** (defined crop
setpoint bands, instrumented sensors, the actuator inventory, the coordinating controller, fault handling)
that keep the loop running and keep every variable in-band between disturbances. A correct decomposition is
graded as a *maintained* invariant ("every climate variable stays within the crop band over time"),
re-generating corrective sub-tasks each cycle — not a graph cycle in the plan (the dependency graph stays a
DAG; the *regime* is what cycles). The richest content (PLAN) is **`Dep`** (actuator×actuator coupling on a
shared environment variable: correcting one variable must not push another out of band) and the
defect-coupling criteria (named, resolved failure couplings of the regime itself).

**Single meta-level.** This decomposes the **control-regime logic** at one level: which variables are
sensed, what each actuator does, how the actuators are coordinated so they do not fight, and how the loop
detects and handles faults. It does **not** spawn org/engineering sub-projects (procure & install the HVAC
hardware, lay out the greenhouse, choose the crop, build the SCADA platform). Components are
capabilities/passes of one control design, not departments or construction tasks. The **crop recipe**
(which setpoints this cultivar wants) is an **input**, not a node to derive (see N1). (Consequence: unlike a
*static* single-meta-level decomposition, this is an inherently **runtime / cyclic** regime — so the
OPERATIONAL failure modes {FM-5,6,7} are genuinely in scope and covered, not N/A-static; see §7.)

## 1. Subtasks `D` — the standing capabilities + the loop passes

> `D` = the children of the decomposition (canon §2.2: `D: T → P(T)`, a DAG).

Each subtask is a **separable capability or loop-pass of the regime** with its own pass/fail criterion on
its *output state* (a sensor reading reflects the true canopy value within tolerance; a heating call raises
air temp toward setpoint within a bound; the controller resolves a setpoint conflict with a defined rule),
**not** a self-report ("heating is installed", "we have sensors"). Per Constitution Ст. II.1.a a criterion
is a predicate over the *outcome* ("with vents commanded open N%, measured RH falls by ≥X within the
interval"), never "X is configured". Sensing (D2) and the controller (D10) are single subtasks — splitting
"the sensor array" into per-probe sub-projects, or the controller into per-rule tickets, collapses to its
parent `D` (single meta-level).

> **Note on the loop vs the standing capabilities.** D2→D10→(D3–D9)→D2 is the running *sense→decide→
> actuate→observe* loop; D1 (bands), the actuator inventory (D3–D9), and D2 (sensing) are the standing
> capabilities the loop draws on each interval to hold every variable in-band over time. Both are required:
> a regime with perfect actuators but no defined bands (no D1) has nothing to control toward; a regime with
> bands and actuators but no coordinating controller (no D10) has each loop fighting the others on the shared
> air. Each is ONE component — splitting "the actuator hardware" into per-device install projects, or the
> controller into per-variable rule tickets, collapses to its parent `D` (single meta-level, and the hardware
> install is N3-excluded).

## 2. Dependencies `Dep` — cross-actuator couplings as explicit, separately-falsifiable `Dep` edges

> `Dep` = the canon primitive (§2.2): an acyclic relation on `T × T`; `(tₐ, tᵦ) ∈ Dep` iff `criteria(tᵦ)`
> references the output of `tₐ` (with coherence `deadline(tₐ) < deadline(tᵦ)`). **Each `Dep` edge is a
> criterion of a child that references a sibling's output — the glue** — and carries its **own** falsifying
> truth-maker (the anti-mock condition), not "these actuators relate" prose. (Item ids keep their original
> X-prefix purely for cross-reference traceability to `reference_buckets_v1.md`; they are all `Dep` edges.)

**The richest and most-forgotten category for T10.** Each seam is a REAL edge: two named actuators act on a
**shared environment variable** (or a sensed variable drives an actuator), and the edge carries its **own**
falsifying criterion — not "these actuators relate" prose (Constitution Ст. II.5 anti-mock). A coupling seam
is covered iff the decomposition names the **same pair of actuators**, the **shared variable they
contend/interact on**, and **a coordination/arbiter rule** (PLAN §1.3 rule 4 — "names vent-cools-but-vents-
CO₂ AND a coordination rule," not "avoids instability"). T10's seams are of two kinds: **(a) actuator×actuator
couplings on a shared variable** (the dominant, richest content — a forgotten one is an objectively
identifiable control conflict / energy waste) and **(b) sense→actuate / cross-variable wiring seams**.

Distinct from V-I6 (the *reading is representative* — outcome) — Dep-X16 is the *mixing actuator/coupling that makes it true* (mechanism); distinct from D4 (vent/fans for *cooling/air-exchange* — Dep-X16 is circulation *for uniformity*, even with no cooling demand). |

> **Anti-mock note.** Each coupling seam references a concrete *shared environment variable*: temperature
> (Dep-X1, Dep-X3b, Dep-X6, Dep-X11), CO₂ (Dep-X2, Dep-X8, Dep-X13), humidity (Dep-X3, Dep-X4, Dep-X5,
> Dep-X12, Dep-X14, Dep-X15), light (Dep-X7, Dep-X8), and the mixed-volume artifact (Dep-X16), and the
> cross-actuator effect is a physically measurable conflict (heating-while-venting wastes measurable energy;
> venting drops measured CO₂; irrigating raises measured RH; fog raises measured RH; a closed screen drives
> under-screen RH to saturation; humidifier-and-dehumidifier both running near one RH wastes measured water/
> energy; an unmixed house holds a measurable inter-zone temp/RH/CO₂ gradient) — a forgotten coupling is an
> objectively identifiable control conflict, not a nominal pair. The wiring seams (Dep-X9 sensor→actuator,
> Dep-X10 the time-of-day regime) reference a concrete binding. None collapse onto each other: different
> actuator pair / shared variable OR different falsifier ⇒ distinct seam (PLAN §1.3 rule 1; canon §2.2
> non-redundancy). In particular the **RH seams** are distinct by *mechanism* and *actuator pair*: the five
> *side-effect* sources — Dep-X3 (vent→RH, *air-exchange*), Dep-X4 (heat→RH, *thermodynamic*), Dep-X5
> (irrigation→RH, *evaporation/transpiration*), Dep-X12 (evap/fog→RH, the *latent-cooler-as-humidifier*,
> falsifier = double-add with the humidifier), Dep-X14 (closed-screen→RH, *trapped-moisture pocket*,
> falsifier = no screen-gap rule) — plus Dep-X15, the *deliberate RH pair opposing itself* (humidify↔dehumidify,
> falsifier = both running near one RH), the RH analogue of Dep-X1 and distinct from all five side-effect
> sources. On CO₂: Dep-X2 (venting *purges* enriched CO₂) ≠ Dep-X8 (enrichment is *pointless without light*,
> the *gate-off* direction) ≠ Dep-X13 (light-on *raises the CO₂ target*, the *enable* direction) — same CO₂×light
> driver, three distinct arcs and falsifiers. On the *latent vs sensible* split: Dep-X11 (evap cools only with
> RH headroom) is the latent cooler's temperature gate, distinct from Dep-X1 (sensible heat↔vent). On
> temperature: Dep-X3b (night screen *radiative* retention) ≠ Dep-X6 (lamp heat) ≠ Dep-X7 (shade *solar-load* +
> light loss). Dep-X16 (HAF mixing *homogenizes the volume*, the precondition for every reading/seam) is the
> lone *spatial-uniformity* seam — it carries the *mechanism* of representativeness that V-I6 carries only as
> an *outcome*, and is distinct from D4 (cooling air-exchange) by being circulation *for uniformity*.

> **FM mapping for `Dep`.** A forgotten/violated `Dep` edge is an **FM-1** defect (a spanning integration
> criterion — the glue — not written / not covered; §4.2 FM-1 insufficiency, §18.10 *hole-i*) when it is a
> coverage hole — the wiring seams Dep-X9 (sense→actuate) and Dep-X10 (day↔night regime) are read this way —
> and an **FM-2** defect (criteria of two children incompatible / a relational defect at fixed composition;
> §4.2 FM-2) for the actuator×actuator coupling seams whose breakage is a mutual-satisfiability conflict on a
> shared variable (Dep-X1–Dep-X8, Dep-X11–Dep-X16 — two actuators that cannot jointly satisfy their criteria
> on the shared variable without an arbiter). For this **maintenance** regime the day↔night transition seam
> additionally touches the **operational** axis: Dep-X10 carries **FM-5** (the setpoint *baseline shifts* at
> dawn/dusk and the regime must reconfigure to it, not run on the stale day/night model). The §7 FM table
> records this. Per one-defect-one-place, a seam's failure is scored **here as a `Dep` item**, never re-listed
> as a separate criterion.

## 3. Authority `Del` — orthogonal note (NOT scored)

> **`Del` is the canon "who" axis** (§2.3: `Del: T → A`, exactly one agent per task) — **orthogonal** to the
> decomposition plane `{D, Dep, V}` and **NOT scored** as a decomposition item. This is a plane-separation
> note, NOT a NEGLECTED exclusion, NOT a criterion, NOT a completeness hole.

**There is no human-approval / sign-off authority node in T10.** Unlike T04 (preparer ≠ approver SoD) or T05
(incident-command authority), the greenhouse control regime has **no org-authority plane to separate out**:
the regime runs as an automated control loop with no human-approval gate inside the decomposition. What
*looks* like authority here — "which actuator owns a shared variable," "one arbiter merges the competing
demands on the vent" — is **not** an org `Del` assignment but a **control rule**: it is a correctness
predicate over the produced control behaviour, carried *inside* the decomposition plane (the controller D10,
the `Dep`-X seams that resolve each coupling, and the **V-I9** single-arbiter-over-shared-actuators
*invariant*). 

**Why the single-arbiter requirement is a criterion, not the orthogonal authority plane (derived).** A GFSO
decomposition is graded on its artifact/behaviour-predicates and how the work splits ({D, Dep, V}). "One
arbiter owns each shared actuator (single-writer)" **is** a predicate over the produced control behaviour —
two loops writing the same vent independently produces measurable fighting/chatter — so it is genuinely a
*correctness criterion* (V-I9), not a `Del` who-assignment. The orthogonal authority plane `Del` (an
emergent edge-relation of the Issuer/Executor delegation hierarchy) carries *who has the org-authority to
set criteria and validate* — a plane that is simply **empty / not exercised** for this fully-automated
regime, not a missing node. There is therefore no SoD-style authority note to lift out of the decomposition;
the only "arbitration" present is the in-plane control invariant V-I9.

**Consequences for scoring (scorer rules):**
- A decomposer is **NOT penalized** for failing to list an org-authority node — there is none to list; the
  arbitration content lives in D10 / the `Dep`-X seams / V-I9, which ARE scored (as decomposition items).
- The single-arbiter requirement is scored as the **criterion V-I9**, not as an authority-plane note. An
  agent who writes "one controller merges all demands on the shared vent/heater" earns **V-I9** criterion
  credit (it is a behaviour predicate), distinct from D10 (the component that does it) and V-I3 (aggregate
  stability) — see the §8 / Appendix adjudication.
- This is **plane-separation by absence** (the `Del` authority plane is unexercised here), not a NEGLECTED
  scope-exclusion and not a completeness hole.

## 4. Criteria `V` — regime predicates (former Invariants + Edge-cases + Failure-modes, COLLAPSED)

> `V` is **derived**: `V(t) = AND(criteria(t))` (§2.2, §3.1 Theorem 1). Every item below is a **predicate of
> the regime** — a `criterion` whose conjunction is the validation. The former Invariants (regime-level
> spanning viability predicates), Edge-cases (boundary-state predicates) and Failure-modes (defect-coupling
> predicates) are **all criteria** and are collapsed here into one section. Each criterion keeps its verbatim
> text + truth-maker and carries an **FM tag**: which canon failure mode(s) it precludes (§4.2 / §4.3 /
> §4.6). The old I/E/F labels are retired — the FM tag replaces them. (Item ids keep an I-/E-/F- prefix
> purely for cross-reference traceability to `reference_buckets_v1.md`; they are NOT separate categories.)

**How to read the FM tag.** Per §4.2/§4.3/§4.6: a **spanning/coverage predicate** a faithful decomposition
must carry (and whose omission is a coverage hole) precludes **FM-1** (Correspondence — joint sufficiency); a
predicate enforcing **mutual satisfiability** of children precludes **FM-2** (Consistency); a predicate
guarding against an **insensitive / gameable / green-but-broken** check (a value that passes where reality
fails) precludes **FM-3** (Verifiability, false-PASS); a predicate enforcing **correct fail-propagation /
ordering / no-stall** precludes **FM-4** (Propagation). For this **maintenance** regime the OPERATIONAL axis
is also in scope: a predicate guarding **stale inputs / mid-run drift** precludes **FM-5** (Currency); a
predicate guarding **a state not-yet-determinable at decision time** precludes **FM-6** (Feasibility); a
predicate guarding the **post-hoc detect→report→fix channel** precludes **FM-7** (Feedback).

### 4.1 Global viability criteria (spanning predicates — the maintenance constraint)

A spanning criterion is a regime-level predicate that **spans actuators** — no single loop establishes it;
it holds only if the whole control regime is jointly correct (canon §2.2 joint sufficiency / §3.1). For a
maintenance goal these are the **viability conditions** the regime must hold *over time*. Canonicalized by
the *conserved quantity / enforced predicate*, not by where it is checked (PLAN §1.3 rule 2; one item per
category).

| # | Name | Conserved quantity / predicate | FM tag (precludes) | Truth-maker (covered iff the decomposition asserts…) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V-I1** | **Every variable stays within its crop band** | At all times, each controlled variable (temp, RH/VPD, CO₂, light, root-zone) is held inside its defined band (D1). The headline maintenance invariant — the viability kernel is never exited. Spans D1–D10. | **FM-1** (spanning coverage: every variable in-band is the joint-sufficiency condition) | …that *every* climate variable is continuously kept inside its target band (not just controlled "on average"). The defining maintenance condition of the task. Credit "keep all variables within the crop's ranges at all times." |
| **V-I2** | **No actuator-induced out-of-bound on another variable (cross-coupling do-no-harm)** | Correcting one variable must not push a *different* variable out of band: venting to cool must not crash CO₂ or RH below band; heating must not over-dry; irrigating must not flood RH. The regime-level "do no harm across the coupling" predicate that all `Dep`-seams serve. | **FM-2** (mutual satisfiability of the actuators' criteria — the global no-cross-harm consistency condition) | …that fixing one variable must not knock another out of band — the global cross-coupling no-harm condition spanning the `Dep`-seams. **The task `spec`'s headline coupling constraint** ("correcting one variable does not push another out of bounds"). |
| **V-I3** | **No two actuators fight / oscillate on the same variable (stability)** | No pair of actuators is driven in opposition on a shared variable, and the closed loops are damped (deadbands, hysteresis, minimum on/off times) so the regime **converges** rather than hunting/oscillating. The aggregate stability of all loops. Spans Dep-X1, Dep-X3, Dep-X15, D10. | **FM-4** (aggregate propagation/stability: the control loops as a whole must converge, not oscillate) | …that the control is *stable* — actuators don't oppose each other or oscillate/hunt on a shared variable (deadbands/hysteresis/anti-hunt). Credit "the loops must be damped / not oscillate / not fight." Distinct from per-pair Dep-X1/Dep-X15 — V-I3 is *aggregate* stability (the named per-pair temp and RH deadbands are Dep-X1/Dep-X15). |
| **V-I4** | **Plant-safety hard limits are never violated** | Beyond the comfort band there are hard limits (max/min temperature, max RH causing condensation/disease, CO₂ worker-safety ppm ceiling, frost) that **must never** be crossed regardless of the comfort objective — a hard-constraint layer overriding the soft setpoint logic. Spans D1, D10, all actuators. | **FM-1** (an inviolable coverage layer no single loop owns) | …that there are *hard plant/worker-safety limits* (frost, lethal heat, CO₂ ppm cap, condensation threshold) that override the comfort control and are never breached. Credit "never let it freeze / cook / exceed the CO₂ safety cap," "hard limits over the setpoint." Distinct from V-I1 (the comfort band) — V-I4 is the inviolable safety layer. |
| **V-I5** | **Energy / resource is not wasted by opposing actions** | The regime does not run actuators against each other (heat+vent simultaneously, enrich while venting, light+shade) — opposing actions waste energy/CO₂/water with no net effect. The conserved quantity is the energy/resource budget; the controller enforces single-direction action per variable. | **FM-2** (mutual satisfiability: opposing actuators on a shared resource cannot jointly satisfy without an arbiter — the no-waste consistency condition) | …that the regime *doesn't waste energy/resource on opposing actions* (no simultaneous heat+cool, no enrich-while-venting). Credit "don't heat and vent at once," "don't waste energy/CO₂ on fighting actuators." Distinct from V-I2 (out-of-band harm) — V-I5 is the *wasted-effort* conservation even when nothing goes out of band. |
| **V-I6** | **Sensed value reflects the canopy-experienced value (control on truth)** | The control acts on readings that actually represent what the crop experiences (calibrated, well-placed, not a single drifting probe) — controlling to a mis-measured value silently steers the real climate out of band. Spans D2, D10. | **FM-3** (false-PASS: a mis-measured reading passes the check where the real climate fails) | …that the control acts on *trustworthy/representative* sensor values (calibration, placement, plausibility/redundancy) — not a single unverified probe. Credit "sensors must be calibrated / representative," "don't control to a bad reading." Distinct from D2 (sensors exist) — V-I6 is the *readings are true* condition; distinct from Dep-X16 (V-I6 is the *outcome* — the reading represents the canopy; Dep-X16 is the *HAF-mixing mechanism* that homogenizes the volume to make it so). |
| **V-I7** | **Accumulated DLI / photoperiod held within the crop band** | Light is an *integrated* target, not an instantaneous one: the regime must hold the **daily light integral and photoperiod** within the crop's range over the whole day — a regime can keep instantaneous PAR "in band" every interval yet miss the day's DLI (too much shading/venting, too short a lit period) or violate the photoperiod (lights on into the dark period). The conserved quantity is the *day's integrated light*, not a snapshot. Spans D1, D8, D9, D10. | **FM-1** (coverage of an *integrated-over-time* target no snapshot check owns) | …that the *accumulated DLI / photoperiod* is held within the crop range over the day (not just instantaneous light in band) — credit "hit the daily light integral," "don't over-shade/over-vent away the light budget," "respect the photoperiod." Distinct from D8 (the lighting actuator) and D1 (the target defined) — V-I7 is the *over-time integrated-light-met* viability condition. |
| **V-I8** | **The control loop stays live (no stall / watchdog)** | For a maintenance regime the loop itself must *keep running*: each control interval the *sense→decide→actuate→observe* cycle completes — a stalled controller, a hung interval, or a frozen schedule silently freezes every actuator at its last command while the climate drifts out of band with nothing correcting it. The conserved predicate is *loop liveness* (a watchdog confirms the cycle closed and commands refreshed each interval). Spans D2, D10, all actuators. | **FM-7** (Feedback / liveness: a stalled loop is a defect with no detect→report channel unless a watchdog carries it) | …that the control loop *keeps running every interval* (liveness / watchdog / no stall) — credit "the loop must not hang / stall," "a watchdog ensures each cycle completes," "detect a frozen controller." The first-class maintenance-liveness invariant (the loop *persisting* is what makes this a maintenance regime). Distinct from V-E5 (a *hardware* failure) — V-I8 is the *control loop itself* staying live. |
| **V-I9** | **A single arbiter owns each shared actuator (no split command authority)** | Where one actuator serves multiple loops (the vent serves temp, RH, and CO₂; the heater serves temp and the heat-vent dehumidification), exactly **one arbiter** (D10) must merge the competing demands into a single command — split authority (two loops each commanding the same vent/heater independently) produces fighting, chatter, and the hidden contention the `Dep`-seams describe. The conserved predicate is *single-writer per actuator*: every shared actuator's command is the output of one merged decision, not concurrent independent loops. Spans D10, Dep-X1–Dep-X16. | **FM-2** (mutual satisfiability: concurrent writers on one actuator cannot jointly satisfy without a single merging arbiter) | …that every *shared* actuator has a *single arbiter* merging the competing loop demands (one command per actuator, not concurrent loops writing the same hardware) — credit "one controller merges the demands on the vent/heater," "no two loops command the same actuator independently." Distinct from V-I3 (aggregate stability/no-oscillation) — V-I9 is the *structural single-writer authority* that makes the merge possible; it is the carrier for the shared-hardware contention the `Dep`-seams expose. |

> **Criteria vs the loop passes.** V-I1–V-I9 are *checked across* the regime but are properties of the whole
> running loop, so they live in **§4 Criteria**, not as internal checks of D10 (no double-count). V-I1 and
> V-I2 are the task `spec`'s two headline conditions (Constitution Ст. II.1.b: criteria must be jointly
> sufficient for `spec`) — V-I1 = "continuously within the viable envelope," V-I2 = "correcting one variable
> does not push another out of bounds"; omitting either leaves the spec uncovered. V-I7–V-I9 are the
> maintenance-regime viability conditions the snapshot checks miss: V-I7 the *integrated* light budget
> (DLI/photoperiod over the day), V-I8 the *loop persisting* (liveness — the defining property of a
> maintenance loop, an OPERATIONAL FM-7 predicate genuinely in-scope here, see §7), V-I9 the *single-writer
> authority* over shared actuators (the structural carrier for the shared-hardware contention in
> Dep-X1–Dep-X16, promoted from D10 prose to a graded invariant). V-I8 and V-I9 are distinct: V-I8 = the loop
> *runs*; V-I9 = when it runs, *one* arbiter owns each shared actuator.

### 4.2 Boundary-state criteria (former Edge-cases)

A boundary-state criterion is a boundary input/state that a naive happy-path regime silently mishandles —
i.e. a predicate the regime must satisfy at the boundary, or the corresponding check is **insensitive** /
**not-yet-determinable** there. Covered iff the decomposition names the same boundary condition and that it
needs a rule.

| # | Name | What it requires | FM tag (precludes) | Truth-maker (covered iff named) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V-E1** | **Extreme outside weather beyond actuator authority** | A heat wave, hard freeze, or storm exceeds what the actuators can offset (vents/cooling can't beat outside heat; heating can't beat extreme cold) — the regime must prioritize the hard-limit defense (protect against frost/cook) and accept a degraded band rather than chase an unreachable setpoint. | **FM-6** (Feasibility: with outside weather beyond actuator authority, the in-band setpoint is *not attainable* — the correct response is a declared degraded-band fallback, not determinable from the normal loop) | …outside weather that *outruns* the actuators (extreme heat/cold/storm) and a defined response (defend the hard limit, accept degraded band, close up / max-cool), not "the actuators handle it." |
| **V-E2** | **Day↔night and sunrise/sunset transition states** | The rapid swing at dawn/dusk (sudden solar gain or loss, screen open/close, dew point crossing) is a boundary the steady-state loops mishandle — needs ramped setpoints and a coordinated transition (this is the *event*; Dep-X10 is the coordination *seam*). | **FM-5** (Currency: the setpoint/baseline regime changes at the transition — the loop must reconfigure to the new day/night baseline, not run on the stale one) | …the dawn/dusk transition as a boundary needing special handling (ramped setpoints, morning dehumidification, screen-opening sequence). Distinct from Dep-X10 (the coordination edge) — V-E2 is the boundary *state* that tests it. |
| **V-E3** | **Rapid external disturbance (sun-through-clouds, wind gust, door open)** | Fast transients — a cloud clearing spikes radiation, a wind gust changes vent effectiveness, an opened door dumps outside air — the regime must damp/rate-limit its response so it doesn't overreact to a transient and induce oscillation. | **FM-4** (propagation/stability at the boundary: a transient must be damped, not propagated into oscillation) | …a rapid transient disturbance (passing cloud / gust / open door) and that the response must be *damped/filtered* so it doesn't overreact. Credit "don't chase a passing cloud," "filter fast transients." |
| **V-E4** | **Saturation / dew-point & condensation onset** | When RH approaches saturation (cold surfaces, night, after irrigation) condensation forms on the canopy/glazing — a boundary where the dehumidification path (heat-and-vent) must engage *before* free water appears, because condensation → disease is then hard to reverse. | **FM-3** (false-PASS: an in-band RH check insensitive to the approach-to-saturation passes while condensation forms) | …the approach-to-saturation / dew-point boundary and a rule that engages dehumidification *before* condensation forms (not after). Distinct from V-F4 (uncorrected humidity→disease as a *failure*) — V-E4 is the boundary the regime must catch. |
| **V-E5** | **Power / equipment failure (degraded actuator set)** | A boiler, fan, vent motor, or the whole power supply fails — the regime must fail to a **safe state** (vents fail-open or fail-closed per the climate, alarm, frost-protection on backup) and keep within the hard limits with a reduced actuator set, not blindly command a dead actuator. | **FM-6** (Feasibility: with an actuator dead the normal command is *not realizable* — needs a declared fail-safe fallback) **+ FM-7** (the failure must raise an alarm/report channel) | …loss of power or an actuator (boiler/fan/vent-motor down) and a fail-safe response (safe default vent position, alarm, frost backup), not assuming all actuators work. |
| **V-E6** | **Crop-stage / setpoint change (the bands themselves move)** | As the crop grows (or a new recipe is loaded) the target bands change — the regime must accept an updated setpoint envelope and re-tune, without a stale band silently controlling to the wrong target. | **FM-5** (Currency: the spec/bands *changed mid-run* — the regime must ingest the updated setpoints or it controls on stale targets) | …that the target bands *change over the crop cycle* (growth stage / new recipe) and the regime ingests the updated setpoints. Distinct from D1 (bands exist) — V-E6 is the band *changing* mid-regime. |
| **V-E7** | **Controller cold-start / restart from unknown actuator state** | On controller (re)start (boot, power-return, software restart) the actuator positions are *unknown* and the climate may be far from band — a naive loop that resumes its last command, or slams actuators to meet the setpoint instantly, induces a transient (cold-dump, overshoot, fighting). The regime needs a defined cold-start: read all sensors, drive actuators to known-safe positions, then *ramp* to setpoint rather than step. Distinct from V-E5 (a *runtime* failure of a running loop) — V-E7 is the *initialization* boundary. | **FM-6** (Feasibility: at cold-start the actuator state is *not yet determinable* — the correct command can't be computed until sensed and driven to a known-safe baseline) | …a defined cold-start/restart sequence (read sensors, actuators to known-safe, ramp not step to setpoint) on controller boot with unknown actuator state. Distinct from V-E5 (runtime actuator failure) — V-E7 is the *startup* transient. |

### 4.3 Defect-coupling criteria (former Failure-modes — only those with NO `Dep`/`I` twin)

Phrased as **concrete coverable claims** (PLAN §1.3 rule 4), never adjectives ("avoids instability" is NOT a
criterion). Each names a specific defect coupling AND its resolution; covered iff the decomposition names the
same coupling and a guard. **One-defect-one-place (PLAN §1.3 rule 1):** a defect-coupling is kept here ONLY
if it has no `Dep`-edge or spanning-invariant twin. The actuator×actuator *fighting/oscillating* couplings
(heat↔vent, vent↔CO₂, the RH sources incl. humidify↔dehumidify, evap-cooling, the screen couplings, mixing)
are carried as `Dep`-seams (Dep-X1–Dep-X16); the aggregate "actuators fight/oscillate" is the **V-I3**
stability invariant, "energy wasted by opposing actions" is **V-I5**, and "two loops command the same
actuator" is the **V-I9** single-arbiter invariant — they are NOT re-listed here (the explicit F-vs-X/I
double-count hazard, PLAN §3 T10 / §4 residual flags). What remains are the regime-level FMs with **no
seam/invariant twin**, each mapped to a canon §4 FM class.

| # | Name | FM tag (precludes) | What it requires (coupling + guard) | Truth-maker (covered iff named with a guard) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V-F1** | **Sensor failure / drift → controlling to a wrong value** | **FM-3** (false-PASS) **+ FM-1** (mis-measured input) | A probe fails or drifts (stuck, miscalibrated, biased) and the controller steers the *real* climate out of band while the reading looks fine — "the gauge says 22°C, the crop is at 30°C." **No `Dep` twin** (Dep-X9 is sense→actuate *wiring*; V-F1 is the wired sensor being *wrong*). Guard: plausibility/range checks, sensor redundancy or cross-check, drift/calibration monitoring, alarm-and-fail-safe on a bad reading. | …that a sensor can *fail or drift* and make the controller steer to a wrong value AND a guard (plausibility check / redundancy / calibration monitoring / fail-safe). Mirrors the false-PASS residual: the reading passes but the climate is wrong. |
| **V-F2** | **Actuator masks a problem instead of fixing it** | **FM-3** (false-PASS) **+ FM-7** (no learning channel) | A response *suppresses the symptom* while the underlying problem persists — e.g. running the dehumidifier flat-out to hide a chronic leak/over-irrigation, or heating continuously to mask a failing screen — the variable reads in-band but the regime is degrading (energy blowout, hidden fault). **No `Dep` twin.** Guard: detect persistent/maxed-out actuator demand as a *fault signal* (root-cause it), not an acceptable steady state. | …that an actuator can *mask* a root problem by running maxed-out (variable looks in-band, fault hidden) AND that persistent saturated actuator demand must raise a fault, not run forever. Credit "constant max heating/dehumidifying means something's broken — alarm it." |
| **V-F3** | **Setpoint conflict between variables with no resolution (controller deadlock/thrash)** | **FM-2** (incompatible criteria) | Two *variable targets* are simultaneously unsatisfiable with the available actuators and the controller has **no priority** — e.g. it's hot and humid: venting cools (good) but the RH/CO₂ targets disagree on venting, so the controller thrashes between conflicting demands. **Distinct from the actuator-pair `Dep`-seams** (those are two actuators on *one* variable; V-F3 is two *variables'* targets demanding opposite actions of *one* actuator with no arbiter). Guard: a defined variable-priority / objective order (e.g. safety > temp > RH > CO₂) so the controller resolves rather than thrashes. | …that two variable *targets* can demand opposite actuator action with no priority (controller thrash/deadlock) AND a defined variable-priority/objective order resolving it. Distinct from Dep-X1–Dep-X5 (one variable, two actuators) — V-F3 is one actuator pulled by two variables. |
| **V-F4** | **Uncorrected humidity → condensation → disease (slow viability loss)** | **FM-1** **+ FM-7** (uncorrected drift, no detection of latent damage) | Chronically high RH that stays *within the soft band but trends to saturation* leads to condensation, then fungal/bacterial disease (Botrytis, mildew) — a slow crop loss the instantaneous in-band check misses. **No `Dep` twin** (the RH *actuator coupling* is Dep-X3/Dep-X4/Dep-X5; V-F4 is the *consequence* of leaving RH uncorrected over time). Guard: control on VPD / dew-point margin (not just RH band), and treat sustained near-saturation as a fault even if "in band." | …that *sustained* high humidity (even within band) causes condensation→disease over time AND a guard (VPD/dew-point control, near-saturation treated as a fault). The slow-viability-loss failure the snapshot check misses. |

> **F-vs-`Dep` / F-vs-`I` discipline (the explicit T10 hazard).** The classic actuator-coupling failures —

## 6. Hardest / most-easily-forgotten seams (flag for scorer)

> Not a category — a scorer pointer at the highest-signal `Dep` edges.

  the representative reading (V-I6 outcome) — is almost never named as a coupling to every variable.
- **Dep-X14** (closed thermal/shade screen *traps moisture under it* → condensation pocket + a humidity-driven
  screen-gap rule) — **among the most-forgotten seams (near-zero typical coverage):** the screen reads as a
  heat/light lever, and the under-screen RH pocket is invisible to a bulk-air sensor; the gap-on-rising-RH rule

- **Dep-X15** (humidify↔dehumidify *direct opposition on RH* → a VPD/RH deadband + mutual-exclusion) — the RH
  analogue of the familiar Dep-X1 temp deadband, yet routinely left to the aggregate "don't oscillate" (V-I3)
  rather than named as the specific RH-pair deadband; an agent that draws the temp deadband often forgets the
  structurally-identical RH one.
- **Dep-X2** (ventilation *purges injected CO₂* + a don't-enrich-while-venting rule) — a parts-list names both
  "ventilation" and "CO₂ injection" but rarely draws the conflict that venting throws away the gas you just
  paid to inject. **Most-forgotten of the CO₂ seams.**
- **Dep-X12** (evaporative/fog cooling *is also a humidifier* + never co-run with the humidifier / suppress near
  the RH ceiling) — evap reads as a pure cooler; that it double-adds moisture and must be a *distinct* RH
  source from vent/heat/irrigation is the forgotten latent-cooler coupling.
- **Dep-X13** (supplemental light *raises/enables the CO₂ target* — the enable arc) — the *opposite* of the
  familiar gate-off-in-dark rule (Dep-X8); a fixed CO₂ setpoint blind to lamp-on under-enriches the lit hours.
- **Dep-X8** (CO₂ enrichment is *useless without light* + gate injection on light) — easily missed because each
  actuator looks fine alone; the *photosynthesis* coupling (CO₂×light) is a cross-actuator usefulness
  constraint, not a single-variable conflict.
- **Dep-X7** (shading *cuts the light the crop needs while cooling* + a heat-vs-light trade rule) — shading reads
  as a pure cooling lever; that it directly fights the photosynthesis/DLI goal (and can trigger supplemental
  lighting, re-adding heat) is the forgotten near-loop.
- **Dep-X10** (the day↔night transition *reconfigures many actuators together* + a coordinated transition rule)
  — a parts-list models steady-state setpoints but drops the dawn/dusk swing where screen-open dumps cold humid
  air and multiple actuators must move in sequence.
- **V-I8 / V-I9** (loop-liveness; single-arbiter-over-shared-actuators) — promoted from prose to graded
  invariants: a maintenance regime that doesn't assert *the loop keeps running* and *one arbiter owns each
  shared actuator* has left two first-class viability conditions ungraded.

## Appendix — settled adjudications carried over (scorer must apply; verbatim from `reference_buckets_v1.md`)

- **D10 controller as a distinct Component.** The coordinating controller is listed as its own component
  (where all `Dep`-couplings are *resolved*) rather than folded into the actuators. Kept **in** because the
  arbitration logic is a separable pass with its own output-criterion (it resolves a setpoint conflict with a
  defined rule) and is exactly where V-I2/V-I3/V-I5 are enforced. Mild risk: a scorer could read "the control
  strategy" as implicit in naming the actuators. **Scorer rule:** credit D10 for a decomposition that names
  the *coordination/arbitration* logic distinctly, not merely "control each variable."
- **V-F3 (variable-target conflict) vs the `Dep`-X actuator-couplings.** Both touch "conflict." Kept distinct
  on a clean truth-maker split: Dep-X1–Dep-X5 = *two actuators* contend on *one* variable (resolved by a
  coordination rule); V-F3 = *two variables'* targets demand opposite action of *one* actuator with no
  priority (resolved by a variable-priority order). An agent can cover one without the other. **Scorer rule:**
  map a "heat and vent fight on temperature" statement → Dep-X1; a "it's hot AND humid and the targets
  disagree, need a priority order" statement → V-F3 — no shared credit.
- **V-I5 (energy waste) vs N4 (cost optimization).** V-I5 (don't run actuators in opposition — the in-band,
  no-waste rule) is in-scope; N4 (full energy-*cost* minimization / off-peak shifting) is the declared
  exclusion. **Scorer rule:** credit "don't heat and vent at once / don't waste energy on fighting" → V-I5,
  and "energy cost optimization / demand-response is out of scope" → N4 — not conflated.
- **V-E5 (runtime actuator/power failure) vs N3 (hardware assumed installed).** V-E5 (a *running* actuator
  fails, fail to safe state) is in-scope; N3 (procuring/installing/sizing the hardware) is excluded. **Scorer
  rule:** credit "a fan/boiler fails, fail-safe and alarm" → V-E5, and "installing/sizing the HVAC is out of
  scope" → N3 — not conflated.
- **Dep-X9 (sense→actuate wiring) vs V-I6 (control on a true reading) vs V-F1 (sensor drift).** Three distinct
  truth-makers on the sensing chain: Dep-X9 = each sensor is *wired to* the correcting actuator (the loop is
  closed); V-I6 = the reading *represents the canopy* (calibrated/placed); V-F1 = the sensor *fails/drifts* and
  a guard catches it. **Scorer rule:** "the controller reads each sensor and drives the matching actuator" →
  Dep-X9; "the sensors must be representative/calibrated" → V-I6; "a stuck/drifting probe must be detected" →
  V-F1 — no shared credit across the three.
- **Dep-X8 (gate CO₂ off in the dark) vs Dep-X13 (raise the CO₂ target under light).** Same pair (CO₂↔light),
  opposite arcs. Dep-X8 = *don't enrich without light* (the safety/waste gate); Dep-X13 = *when light is on,
  raise/enable the CO₂ target* (the exploit/enable direction). An agent can name the gate without the enable.
  **Scorer rule:** "no CO₂ in the dark" → Dep-X8; "CO₂ setpoint tracks/rises with active light" → Dep-X13 — no
  shared credit.
- **Dep-X12 (evap/fog→RH) vs the other RH sources (Dep-X3/Dep-X4/Dep-X5/Dep-X14).** Evap/fog is a *distinct
  actuator* that raises RH; its falsifier is *double-adding moisture by co-running with the humidifier*,
  distinct from vent→RH (Dep-X3), heat→RH (Dep-X4), irrigation→RH (Dep-X5), and trapped-under-screen RH
  (Dep-X14). **Scorer rule:** "fog cools but also humidifies, don't run it with the humidifier" → Dep-X12 —
  not folded into the others.
- **Dep-X11 (evap-cooling temperature gate) vs Dep-X1 (sensible heat↔vent).** Dep-X11 is the *latent* cooler's
  gate (cool only with RH headroom, after sensible cooling exhausted); Dep-X1 is the *sensible* heat-vs-vent
  deadband. **Scorer rule:** "fog only when vents maxed AND RH below ceiling" → Dep-X11; "don't heat and vent
  at once" → Dep-X1 — distinct.
- **Dep-X15 (humidify↔dehumidify deadband) vs the side-effect RH seams (Dep-X3/Dep-X4/Dep-X5/Dep-X12/Dep-X14)
  and vs V-I3/V-I5.** Dep-X15 is the *deliberate RH pair opposing itself* on one variable (the RH analogue of
  Dep-X1's temp deadband); Dep-X3/Dep-X4/Dep-X5/Dep-X12/Dep-X14 are *other* actuators that move RH as a *side
  effect*. And Dep-X15 is the *specific* deadband on this one pair, where V-I3 (aggregate no-oscillation) and
  V-I5 (aggregate no-waste) are the *regime-wide* invariants — **no shared credit:** an agent that names "the
  humidifier and dehumidifier need a deadband so they don't fight on RH" earns Dep-X15; a generic "don't let
  the loops oscillate / waste energy" earns V-I3/V-I5, not Dep-X15. **Scorer rule:** map the *named RH-pair
  deadband* → Dep-X15; the *aggregate stability/waste* statement → V-I3/V-I5; a *side-effect* RH source → its
  own `Dep`-X. Falsifier for Dep-X15 = both RH actuators running near one RH; the others' falsifiers are
  distinct.
- **Dep-X16 (HAF mixing↔every variable) vs V-I6 (representative reading) vs D4 (cooling fans).** Dep-X16 is the
  *mixing actuator/coupling that produces uniformity* (the mechanism: run HAF so temp/RH/CO₂ are homogeneous
  and every loop controls the bulk air); V-I6 is the *outcome* (the reading represents the canopy); D4 is the
  vent/fan for *cooling/air-exchange*. **No shared credit:** "the sensors must be representative/calibrated" →
  V-I6 (outcome); "run circulation/HAF fans to keep the air mixed and avoid stratified pockets" → Dep-X16
  (mechanism); "run exhaust/vent fans to cool / exchange air" → D4. **Scorer rule:** an agent can assert V-I6
  (control on a true reading) without ever naming the *mixing actuator* that makes one reading true — Dep-X16
  is credited only when the homogenizing-circulation seam is named. Falsifier for Dep-X16 = point-sensor
  control with no mixing actuator (stratified pockets uncorrected). **GT ranks this the most-forgotten
  coupling of all.**
- **Dep-X3b (night-screen radiative retention↔heating) vs Dep-X7 (shade↔light) vs Dep-X6 (lamp heat).** All
  touch the screen/light/heat region but differ by lever and shared variable: Dep-X3b = the *night thermal
  screen retains radiative heat, lowering heating demand* (temp, radiative); Dep-X7 = the *shade screen cuts
  PAR while cooling* (light + temp, solar); Dep-X6 = *lamp heat* (temp). **Scorer rule:** "close the screen at
  night to hold heat / don't over-fire under it" → Dep-X3b; "shading cools but starves the light" → Dep-X7;
  "lamps add heat" → Dep-X6 — no overlap.
- **V-I8 (loop-liveness) vs V-E5 (hardware failure) vs V-I9 (single-arbiter).** V-I8 = the *control loop
  itself* keeps cycling (watchdog, no stall) — a software/scheduling liveness predicate; V-E5 = a *hardware*
  actuator/power failure (fail to safe state); V-I9 = *one arbiter owns each shared actuator* (single-writer
  structure). A regime can have live hardware (no V-E5) yet a stalled controller (V-I8 fail), or a running
  loop (V-I8 ok) with two loops fighting over the same vent (V-I9 fail). **Scorer rule:** "the loop must not
  hang / a watchdog refreshes commands" → V-I8; "a fan/boiler dies, fail-safe" → V-E5; "one controller merges
  all demands on the shared vent/heater" → V-I9 — three distinct credits.
- **V-I9 (single-arbiter) vs V-I3 (no oscillation) vs D10 (the controller component).** V-I9 = the
  *structural* single-writer authority over each shared actuator; V-I3 = the *dynamic* aggregate stability
  (loops don't hunt/oscillate); D10 = the *component* that does the arbitration. V-I9 makes the merge
  *possible*; V-I3 is the merged result being *stable*; D10 is the *part* doing it. **Scorer rule:** "no two
  loops command the same actuator" → V-I9; "the loops are damped / don't hunt" → V-I3; "the
  coordination/arbitration logic" → D10 — no collapse.
