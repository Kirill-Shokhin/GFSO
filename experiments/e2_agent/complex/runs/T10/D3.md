## D — Subtasks / Components

**D1 — Setpoint manager**: Maintains all CV setpoints (air T, root-zone T, RH, CO₂, PAR, moisture/EC/pH) as functions of time-of-day and crop growth stage using ramps (not steps) at transitions; advances crop stage on schedule or operator confirmation; defines day/night boundary via astronomical sunrise/sunset (not supplemental-light state); includes a named empty-greenhouse / harvest-transition mode in which CO₂ injection, irrigation, and supplemental lighting are suspended and only maintenance-temperature setpoints apply — mode entered at crop removal and cleared on confirmed replanting.

**D2 — Per-CV feedback controllers**: One closed-loop controller (PID or equivalent, with anti-windup) per controlled variable — air temperature, root-zone temperature (floor heating or substrate pipes, independently sensed and separately actuated), RH, CO₂, PAR/DLI (tracking both instantaneous flux and accumulated daily light integral), root-zone moisture + EC + pH (jointly; moisture and EC from substrate sensors, pH from in-line or drain-stream pH probe with acid/base dosing actuator). The pH controller enforces a minimum inter-dose interval or hold-after-dose period sufficient for the substrate to equilibrate before re-sampling (see V42). Each controller reads D5-validated sensor values and drives its primary actuator with a continuous (not binary) manipulated variable.

**D3 — Interlock / coupling rule engine**: The arbitration layer above individual controllers. Prevents one controller's output from violating another CV's bounds by implementing all identified two-way and three-way coupling rules (including the cold × humid × CO₂-depleted conflict), enforcing actuator mechanical limits (vent aperture vs. wind speed, screen position vs. vent operability), and providing a total priority ordering under combined stress. Includes a defined VPD-correction rule specifying which actuator is commanded, at what priority, and at what magnitude when VPD computed by D5 falls outside crop bounds while individual T and RH readings remain within their respective setpoints (so that a condition where all per-CV readings pass while VPD fails does not go without response). When two or more D2 controllers simultaneously command conflicting positions on the same physical actuator, D3 resolves the conflict via a single, explicit aggregation rule (priority order, min-wins, or weighted blend) — last-write-wins is not acceptable. Receives real-time actuator positions from independent feedback, not only commanded values. Receives safe-state override commands from D4 and enforces them as the highest-priority constraint on all actuator arbitration — normal control-loop outputs are subordinated to an active D4 safe-state command.

**D4 — Alarm and safety layer**: Monitors all CVs and actuator states independently of the control layer; monitors HAF (horizontal airflow fan) running state and fires an alarm when any fan bank is not confirmed running (triggering D5 flagging of spatial-sampling validity); fires alarms for bound violations, heating/cooling/lighting/CO₂-injection saturation, CO₂ worker-safety exceedance (> 5 000 ppm), sensor faults, supply failures, and progressive-degradation alerts (heating efficiency, DLI accumulation rate, leaching fraction) sourced from D6 trend outputs; activates documented safe-state fallbacks by issuing explicit override commands to D3 to enforce actuator safe positions. Must remain operational when any individual controller fails.

**D5 — Sensor validation layer**: Detects stuck readings, out-of-range values, and rate-of-change anomalies for all sensors; substitutes calibrated fallback values or raises faults before values reach D2 or D4. Receives HAF fan running state from the actuator-state bus; when any fan bank is not confirmed running, D5 flags affected spatial-sampling channels as potentially unrepresentative and routes that flag to D4 (which fires a fan-failure alarm per V39) rather than continuing to accept single-point readings as representative. Derives VPD from validated air T and RH and routes the computed value to D1 and D3 — VPD is never derived from raw sensor reads anywhere in the control path. Anomaly detection thresholds are parameterised by the current crop stage supplied by D1 (seedling vs. mature canopy vs. empty-greenhouse mode); a reading valid for one stage must not trigger a false fault at another. Maintains cross-check or calibration-drift schedule for drift-prone classes (RH, CO₂ NDIR, PAR quantum, soil EC, pH probe). Requires adequate horizontal airflow (mixing fans) as a precondition for sensor representativeness; HAF fan running state is an explicit input, not an assumed ambient condition.

**D6 — Logging / historian**: Time-series storage of all sensor readings, actuator states, and derived metrics (VPD, DLI accumulation, leaching fraction, heating thermal efficiency) at ≥ 1 min resolution. Exposes trend-aggregated outputs (progressive efficiency loss, EC trend, drain-to-feed ratio, DLI accumulation rate) to D4 for progressive-degradation alarm evaluation; raw storage without a named D4-consumer for trend analysis is insufficient.

**D7 — Feedforward / weather-station integration**: Processes outdoor-station data (T, RH, wind speed/direction, global radiation/pyranometer, dewpoint derived) through a plausibility validation step — applying per-channel range bounds, rate-of-change limits, and time-of-day cross-checks (e.g., pyranometer reading against solar-angle model, anemometer stability pattern) — before feeding validated or flagged outdoor signals downstream. A channel that passes communication checks but fails plausibility triggers the same safe-mode fallback as V32 communication loss (see V40). Validated feedforward signals are consumed by D1 (astronomical day boundary), D3 (vent limits, humidity-inversion strategy, three-way conflict inputs), D2 T channel (anticipatory T compensation), and D2 irrigation channel (ET-model–derived anticipatory deficit signal computed from cumulative outdoor radiation and crop water-use coefficients, triggering irrigation before sensor-detected root-zone deficit). Outdoor station is a hard system dependency; communication loss triggers V32 fallback.

## Dep — Cross-Component Dependency Seams

**Dep1 — D1 → D2 (setpoint propagation)**: All controller setpoints originate from D1 and propagate to D2 at each schedule transition. Broken: setpoints hard-coded per-controller; crop-stage change requires editing N controllers independently with inconsistent results.

**Dep2 — D7 → D1 (astronomical day boundary)**: D1's day/night transition time is sourced from outdoor global radiation or an astronomical clock, not from supplemental-light state. Broken: on an overcast day with supplemental lights on all day, the night T setback never fires; DIF is lost.

**Dep3 — D5 → D2 (validated sensor values to controllers)**: Controllers receive D5-validated readings; raw sensor values never reach D2. Broken: a stuck humidity sensor at 30 % keeps vents closed in 95 % RH conditions.

**Dep4 — D5 → D4 (validated sensor values to alarms)**: Alarm comparisons use D5-validated readings. Broken: a raw-stuck sensor suppresses alarms or triggers false ones at D4.

**Dep5 — D3 → D2 (interlock override of controller outputs)**: D3 can override, limit, or blend individual D2 controller outputs before they reach actuators. Broken: controllers run unconstrained; ventilation for RH drives T below LTL with no interlock.

**Dep6 — D7 → D3 (outdoor conditions to interlock)**: D3 receives outdoor T, RH, dewpoint, wind speed, and global radiation from D7 to evaluate vent limits, humidity-inversion strategy, and heating-capacity margin. Broken: interlock is purely reactive to indoor state; a cold front or fog event is not anticipated; T crashes before heating can compensate.

**Dep7 — Actuator-state bus → D3 (real-time positions)**: D3 receives current vent aperture, screen position (verified, not commanded), lighting on/off and dimming level, and heating pipe temperature from independent position feedback. Broken: D3 operates on commanded rather than actual positions; screen encoder slip or vent obstruction is invisible to arbitration logic.

**Dep8 — D1 clock → all timed transitions**: All setpoint transitions (day/night, crop-stage advance, empty-greenhouse mode entry/exit) are governed by a single, DST-aware, leap-second-aware clock source shared across D1–D4. Broken: per-controller clocks or unhandled DST cause transitions to shift one hour; DIF disrupted; CO₂ injection starts in darkness.

**Dep9 — D5 (derived VPD) → D1, D3**: D5 computes VPD from validated air T and RH and routes the result to D1 (VPD-aware setpoint context) and D3 (humidity-inversion evaluation and coupling-rule inputs, including VPD-response rule). VPD is never computed from raw sensor readings anywhere in the control path. Broken: a temporarily faulty RH sensor causes D3 to evaluate humidity-inversion strategy on an incorrect VPD; D3 opens vents on a foggy morning; indoor RH spikes.

**Dep10 — Lighting actuator state → D2 T channel (disturbance feedforward)**: The lighting on/off and dimming state (from the actuator-state bus) feeds a disturbance-compensation signal directly into D2's T controller (feedforward offset or gain adjustment for the HPS ≈ 60 % convective heat load). This is distinct from Dep7, which routes lighting state to D3 for interlock arbitration. Broken: lights activate; T rises above UTL before the PID reacts because no feedforward compensates for the thermal disturbance; V28 cannot be satisfied.

**Dep11 — D1 crop stage → D5 (anomaly detection thresholds)**: D5's valid-range and rate-of-change bounds for each sensor channel are parameterised by the current crop stage and mode supplied by D1 (including empty-greenhouse). Broken: at a crop-stage advance, a CO₂ reading valid for the new stage triggers a false fault because D5 still applies seedling thresholds; or post-harvest, a dry-substrate reading is treated as a sensor fault rather than an expected empty-greenhouse condition.

**Dep12 — D6 historian → D4 (trend analysis for progressive-degradation alarms)**: D6's stored thermal output, EC, drain-to-feed ratio, and DLI accumulation data are aggregated into trend metrics consumed by D4 for progressive-degradation alarms (V31 heating efficiency, V36 lamp bank, V37 leaching fraction). Broken: D6 accumulates thermal logs for six months; no consumer reads them; progressive pipe fouling degrades heating output by 20 % before the T setpoint becomes unreachable.

**Dep13 — D2 CO₂ channel saturation state → D4 (injection-saturation alarm)**: D2's CO₂ controller monitors valve-at-maximum duration and signals D4 when the configurable hold threshold is exceeded, triggering the injection-saturation alarm (V25). Broken: CO₂ valve stays fully open for 36 hours without reaching setpoint; no alarm fires because D2 and D4 each expect the other to detect saturation.

**Dep14 — HAF fan state → D5 (spatial-sampling validity flag)**: The running state of each HAF fan bank (from the actuator-state bus or dedicated current/speed monitor) is an explicit input to D5 so D5 can flag spatial-sampling as potentially invalid when fans are not confirmed running, rather than silently treating single-point readings as representative. Broken: one HAF bank trips; D5 continues accepting single-point sensor reads as spatially representative; a 4 °C cross-span temperature gradient goes undetected; V21 and V39 cannot be satisfied.

**Dep15 — D4 → D3 (safe-state override command path)**: When D4 activates a documented fallback (e.g., heating-saturation fallback, CO₂ worker-safety cut, post-power-restoration sequencing), it issues an explicit override command to D3 specifying the required actuator positions; D3 treats the D4 override as highest-priority input, subordinating normal control-loop outputs. Broken: D4 fires a heating-saturation alarm (V12); D3, receiving no override signal, leaves a vent at 30 % aperture commanded by RH control; indoor T continues falling below LTL despite the alarm.

**Dep16 — D2 irrigation channel → D2 root-zone T channel (cold-water disturbance feedforward)**: Feed water temperature (or a season-calibrated cold-water flag) from the irrigation channel feeds a disturbance-compensation signal to the root-zone T controller before large irrigation events execute, so the root-zone T controller can pre-compensate rather than react after substrate temperature has already dropped. Broken: large morning irrigation event delivers 5 °C feed water to a substrate at 20 °C setpoint; root-zone T drops 3 °C below LTL in 12 min; floor heating saturates trying to recover; V44 fires with no upstream warning.

## V — Criteria (decidable predicates over the produced result)

**V1 — Simultaneous viability envelope**: At all sampled instants, all seven CVs (air T, root-zone T, RH, CO₂, PAR, root-zone moisture + EC, pH) and derived VPD are within crop bounds simultaneously. A design that keeps each CV individually in range but allows VPD, root-zone T, or pH to drift out of bounds fails this criterion.

**V2 — Coupling-rule completeness**: Every identified two-way and three-way combination of conflicting actuator demands — including simultaneous competing demands on the same physical actuator — has exactly one defined resolution rule with a total priority ordering; no combination results in undefined behavior or oscillation. A design without a cold × humid × CO₂-depleted three-way rule, or without an explicit single-actuator aggregation rule in D3, fails.

**V3 — Actuator capacity feasibility at design conditions**: Heating and cooling setpoints are demonstrably achievable by installed plant on the site's design-day outdoor conditions; the design includes a capacity calculation verifying this. A design whose T setpoint is physically unreachable at design-day conditions fails.

**V4 — CO₂ setpoint reachability under vent-state constraints**: The CO₂ enrichment setpoint is achievable given injection rate, greenhouse volume, vent-aperture leakage, and photosynthetic sink; the design explicitly quantifies the vent-state at which CO₂ injection becomes ineffective and defines the fallback. A design that sets 1 200 ppm with vents fully open fails.

**V5 — Water / EC balance across a growth cycle**: The irrigation strategy maintains substrate moisture and EC within crop bounds over a full growth cycle without EC accumulation to osmotic-stress levels; design includes a drainage-fraction target and an EC-trend alarm. A design with no drainage-fraction check or EC trend monitoring fails.

**V6 — Photoperiod integrity**: Cumulative light-on hours per 24 h match the crop's photoperiod requirement, accounting for both supplemental-lighting extension and shading curtailment of natural light. A design where full shade closure shortens the photoperiod without supplemental compensation fails.

**V7 — DIF constraint satisfaction**: Day and night T setpoints jointly satisfy the DIF (day – night temperature difference) target for the crop's morphological requirements. Adjusting day T without a corresponding night-T review fails this criterion.

**V8 — Schedule continuity**: The setpoint schedule has zero gaps or double-execution events at midnight boundaries, crop-stage transitions, empty-greenhouse mode entry/exit, DST changes, and leap-second events. Any period with an uncovered schedule block fails.

**V9 — Sensor fault isolation and fallback**: A single sensor failure (stuck, out-of-range, dropout) triggers a switch to a defined safe fallback value in D2 without propagating the faulty reading to other controllers or to D4. The system must not continue on bad data silently.

**V10 — Fail-safe actuator positions**: All actuators have specified, tested, and documented fail-safe positions for power loss that prevent immediate crop loss across the site's climate range; the positions are mechanically enforced or firmware-resident, not software-dependent.

**V11 — CO₂ worker-safety cut-off**: An interlock independent of the crop control software and its sensors cuts CO₂ injection and triggers an audible alarm when concentration exceeds 5 000 ppm; a sensor fault in the crop controller that reads low does not defeat this interlock.

**V12 — Heating-saturation alarm and fallback**: When the heating plant reaches maximum rated output and indoor T remains below LTL, a saturation alarm fires within one control cycle and a documented minimum-T fallback activates; silent crop loss at heating limits is not acceptable.

**V13 — Cooling-saturation alarm**: When full vent aperture combined with full shading cannot maintain UTL, an overtemperature alarm fires with a documented crop-heat-stress threshold; the regime does not continue silently into crop damage.

**V14 — Outdoor humidity inversion handling**: When outdoor dewpoint ≥ indoor dewpoint, the humidity strategy does not open vents for RH reduction; it switches to an alternative (heating-assisted drying, or temporary RH tolerance) and alarms if no effective alternative is available. Opening vents on a foggy morning that raises indoor RH fails this criterion.

**V15 — Thermal-screen / humidity compatibility (full closure)**: When the thermal screen is fully closed at night, RH is managed without relying on full roof ventilation; the design specifies an effective alternative (calibrated minimum vent gap, heating-based drying) that does not mechanically conflict with the screen. A design where the only humidity actuator is vent-open under a closed screen fails.

**V16 — Anti-windup in all closed-loop controllers**: No D2 controller allows unbounded integral accumulation during actuator saturation; recovery from saturation does not produce a CV overshoot beyond the opposite limit. A T overshoot past UTL immediately after a cold-snap ends (integral windup) fails this criterion.

**V17 — Sensor calibration drift detection**: Each drift-prone sensor class (RH, CO₂ NDIR, PAR quantum, soil EC, pH probe) has either a secondary cross-check or a maintenance schedule that bounds maximum undetected drift to within 50 % of the allowable error band. A design with no calibration schedule and no cross-check fails.

**V18 — Actuator position verification**: Screen position, vent aperture, valve states, and acid/base dosing pump delivery are independently verified against end-stops, torque sensors, secondary encoders, or flow confirmation; reported position is not taken as authoritative without independent confirmation. A design where screen drive slip is undetectable fails.

**V19 — CO₂ supply failure detection**: A CO₂ mass-flow meter or supply-line pressure sensor detects supply exhaustion independently of the concentration sensor; injection-valve open with zero confirmed flow triggers a supply-failure alarm. A design where supply runs out undetected for days fails.

**V20 — Emitter-level flow confirmation**: Irrigation delivery is confirmed at or downstream of the emitter (flow meter, substrate moisture rate-of-change cross-check); a partial or full emitter clog does not result in the controller silently continuing to demand irrigation while the root zone dries.

**V21 — Spatial sensing coverage**: Sensor placement spans the greenhouse zone variation (multiple spans, proximity to vents and heat pipes); single-point sensing is rejected where zone T or RH variation exceeds 50 % of the allowable deviation from setpoint. Horizontal airflow fans must be operational as a precondition for sensor representativeness; their operation is interlinked with sensing validity.

**V22 — Rain / soiling suppression of PAR-triggered lighting**: Supplemental lighting triggered by a low PAR reading is cross-checked against outdoor global radiation (pyranometer) and/or a rain flag; a false-low reading from rain droplets or sensor soiling does not cause unnecessary lighting activation that pushes T above UTL.

**V23 — Wind-speed vent limit**: Vent aperture is hard-constrained (in D3) by an outdoor anemometer threshold to protect against structural damage and uncontrolled cold infiltration; this is a non-negotiable upper bound on aperture, not a soft advisory. A design where vent actuators ignore wind speed above a structural threshold fails.

**V24 — CO₂ injection gated on photosynthetic sink**: CO₂ injection is active only when PAR exceeds the photosynthetic compensation point and canopy uptake can absorb enrichment; injection during darkness, under heavy shading (> 95 % screen closure), or in empty-greenhouse mode is blocked. Nighttime injection or post-harvest injection that accumulates CO₂ with no agronomic gain fails.

**V25 — CO₂ injection-saturation alarm**: When the CO₂ injection valve has been at maximum for a configurable hold period without the setpoint being reached, an "injection saturation" alarm fires (indicating structural infiltration leak or supply issue); the valve does not simply remain open indefinitely at steady state.

**V26 — Irrigation day/night interlock**: Irrigation is inhibited during the dark period regardless of substrate moisture sensor readings; the interlock is in the control execution path (not advisory) so nighttime threshold exceedance does not fire the drip system and spike nocturnal RH.

**V27 — Heating pipe surface temperature above dewpoint**: The heating pipe temperature setpoint specifies a minimum pipe surface T relative to local air dewpoint during high-humidity conditions, not just an absolute value; condensation drip onto canopy from pipes below dewpoint fails this criterion.

**V28 — Supplemental lighting heat load in T control**: When supplemental lights activate, the T control channel receives a feedforward offset or gain adjustment reflecting the thermal load from lighting (HPS ≈ 60 % convective heat); light activation does not trigger a T alarm without a heat-load compensation mechanism.

**V29 — DLI accumulation tracking**: The PAR control channel tracks both instantaneous flux (µmol m⁻² s⁻¹) and daily accumulated light integral (mol m⁻² d⁻¹); supplemental lighting extension is gated on DLI completion state for the day, not only on instantaneous PAR. A design that cannot decide when supplemental lighting is done for the day fails.

**V30 — Canopy-to-air temperature correction under radiation load**: In high-radiation conditions the design specifies either a canopy-temperature sensor or a radiation-load correction model; control declared compliant while the canopy surface is 3–8 °C above air T fails this criterion.

**V31 — Heating plant performance monitoring**: A metric (effective thermal output vs. demand) is logged and trended via D6 → D4; a ≥ 10 % progressive drop in delivery efficiency (pipe fouling, flow restriction) triggers a maintenance alert before T setpoint becomes unreachable. A design with no performance metric allows silent actuator degradation.

**V32 — Outdoor station communication failure fallback**: When D7 loses communication with the outdoor weather station, D3 switches immediately to documented conservative safe-mode defaults for all weather-dependent limits (vent aperture locked at a safe maximum, humidity strategy defaults to heating-assisted drying, wind-speed vent limit set to zero override); the degraded state triggers an alarm and does not silently drop any weather-dependent constraint. A design where anemometer comms failure removes the wind-speed vent limit without alarm fails.

**V33 — Power-restoration sequencing**: On restoration of power following an outage, actuator re-energization follows a defined sequencing protocol preventing simultaneous conflicting demands (heating, lighting heat load, vents, CO₂ injection all firing together); T overshoot beyond UTL within 10 minutes of restoration fails this criterion.

**V34 — Manual override detection and return-to-auto**: When the independently verified actuator position persistently diverges from the commanded position beyond a configurable hold time, the affected channel is flagged as manually overridden, an alarm is raised, and automatic control on that channel is suspended until an explicit operator-confirmed return-to-auto event clears the flag. A design where a manually opened vent is invisible to the control system — D3 sees commanded-closed matching encoder-closed due to local override while T crashes — fails.

**V35 — Actuator travel-time transient constraint**: D3 evaluates and enforces interlocks against intermediate actuator positions throughout the full travel stroke, not only at the commanded final position; a vent commanded from 10 % to 100 % aperture is subject to wind-speed limits at every intermediate position during travel. A design where interlocks are evaluated only at the final commanded position fails.

**V36 — Supplemental lamp bank failure detection**: When supplemental lighting actuators are at maximum demand for a configurable hold period and the DLI accumulation rate drops below 50 % of the expected rate for the prevailing outdoor radiation level, a lamp-bank failure alarm fires. The system does not accumulate DLI deficit for more than one day without detection; silent failure analogous to heating saturation (V12) or CO₂ injection saturation (V25) is not acceptable.

**V37 — Drain EC vs. feed EC monitoring (leaching fraction)**: Drain EC and feed EC are measured independently; leaching fraction (drain EC ÷ feed EC) is logged and triggers an under-leaching alarm below the minimum acceptable threshold. In-substrate EC alone is insufficient — a design monitoring only in-slab EC misses salt accumulation from under-leaching because in-slab reads a blend of fed and accumulated salts.

**V38 — CO₂ NDIR positive-drift online detection**: The CO₂ control path includes an indicator that distinguishes a genuinely satisfied setpoint (injection valve closed, crop growth consistent with adequate CO₂) from an upward-drift state (injection valve closed, growth or secondary indicators inconsistent with adequate CO₂); the calibration cross-check interval bounds the maximum undetected drift window. A design where the sensor reads 200 ppm above the true concentration indefinitely — causing the controller to under-inject while V25's saturation alarm never fires because the valve is not at maximum — fails this criterion.

**V39 — HAF fan failure alarm and sensor-validity flag**: When any HAF fan bank is not confirmed running (via current draw, shaft speed, or equivalent), D4 fires a fan-failure alarm and D5 flags spatial-sampling on affected sensor channels as potentially invalid; the system does not silently continue treating single-point indoor sensor readings as spatially representative with fans down. A design where a fan-bank failure is undetectable — allowing a 4 °C cross-span temperature gradient to go undetected while V21 is nominally satisfied — fails this criterion.

**V40 — Outdoor sensor plausibility validation**: D7 applies per-channel plausibility checks (range bounds, rate-of-change limits, cross-checks against time-of-day models or redundant channels) before passing outdoor data downstream; a sensor reading that passes communication integrity checks but fails plausibility — an iced anemometer locked at 2 m/s in a 15 m/s gust, or a soiled pyranometer reading zero at solar noon — triggers the same safe-mode fallback and alarm as a V32 communication loss. A design that passes implausible-but-communicating outdoor readings into D3 or D1 unchecked fails this criterion.

**V41 — VPD closed-loop response rule**: When VPD computed by D5 falls outside crop bounds while individual air T and RH readings are each within their respective setpoints, D3 invokes a defined VPD-correction rule specifying which actuator is commanded, at what magnitude, and at what priority relative to other active interlock constraints; the response rule exits with a total priority ordering and cannot resolve to no action. A design where VPD can be out of bounds (e.g., 2.6 kPa above the crop limit) while both T and RH controllers report on-setpoint and no controller responds fails this criterion.

**V42 — pH dosing equilibration protocol**: The pH controller enforces a minimum inter-dose interval or explicit hold-after-dose period sufficient for substrate equilibration (commensurate with the substrate's measured response lag, typically minutes to tens of minutes) before issuing a subsequent dose command; the next dose is contingent on sensor stabilization or the parameterized wait-time expiring, not on the next control-loop sample. This criterion is distinct from V16 (anti-windup): V16 addresses actuator saturation; V42 addresses repeated dosing without saturation when the physical equilibration lag exceeds the controller's sampling period. A controller that re-samples and re-doses before the previous dose equilibrates — crashing pH below 4.5 while the valve was never at maximum — fails this criterion.

**V43 — Acid/base dosing pump delivery confirmation**: Dosing pump delivery is independently confirmed by a flow sensor or by a pH rate-of-change cross-check matching the expected dose-response magnitude; a stuck pump that accumulates maximum demand without confirmed delivery triggers an alarm before the next enabled delivery event. A design where a pump that has been stuck closed for two hours delivers its full accumulated demand on mechanical clearance — crashing pH from 6.0 to below 4.0 in a single event — fails this criterion.

**V44 — Root-zone T saturation alarm (cold-water irrigation)**: When the root-zone heating plant (floor heating or substrate pipes) reaches maximum rated output and root-zone T remains below LTL — in particular following a large cold-water irrigation event — a saturation alarm fires within one control cycle and a documented fallback activates; silent crop loss from root-zone cold shock is not acceptable. This criterion is the root-zone analogue of V12 (air heating saturation) and is distinct because root-zone T is independently actuated and V12's trigger condition does not cover root-zone thermal saturation.

**V45 — Thermal screen partial-closure RH stratification**: When the thermal screen is at an intermediate position (nominally 20–80 % closed), the design specifies either (a) a below-screen sensor capturing canopy-level RH, or (b) a documented correction applied to above-screen or gutter-level readings to account for the RH stratification boundary created by the partially deployed screen; a design relying solely on above-screen sensing to certify canopy-level RH during partial-screen operation — allowing disease-enabling conditions to accumulate below the screen while the monitored reading shows compliance — fails this criterion. This criterion is distinct from V15, which addresses actuator compatibility under full screen closure.

## N — Declared Scope Exclusions

**N1 — Pest and disease management**: Biological control agents and pesticide application are out of scope. IN scope: any CV setpoint defined (partly) to prevent enabling conditions for disease (RH < 90 %, condensation avoidance) — those setpoints are part of the control regime.

**N2 — Fertilizer chemistry (N:P:K recipe)**: Nutrient formulation is out of scope. IN scope: EC and pH as irrigation control targets, their sensors, their dosing actuators, and their coupling to the moisture controller (all covered by D2).

**N3 — Greenhouse structural design**: Frame, glazing, and screen structural integrity are out of scope. IN scope: mechanical limits on vent aperture and screen travel speed that constrain actuator setpoints in D3.

**N4 — Long-term crop scheduling and harvest planning**: Growth scheduling and harvest logistics are out of scope. IN scope: current crop-stage setpoints and their automatic advancement at defined transitions, including entry into and exit from the empty-greenhouse maintenance mode (covered by D1).

**N5 — Grid / energy market optimization**: Tariff-based demand-curtailment and peak-shifting are out of scope for the core regime. If added, they introduce constraints on heating and lighting that must be reconciled with CV bounds — that reconciliation would then be in scope.

**N6 — CO₂ source generation**: Boiler management, cylinder logistics, and flue-gas treatment are out of scope. IN scope: supply availability monitoring (pressure sensor, low-supply alarm, flow measurement) as part of D4, because supply failure is a silent failure mode.

**N7 — Worker access and building management**: Occupational scheduling and access control are out of scope. IN scope: the CO₂ worker-safety cut-off interlock (V11) as a mandatory hardware interlock on the CO₂ controller.
