## D — Subtasks / Components

**D1. Plan & period model** — Represent each plan as (amount, currency, billing_period ∈ {monthly, annual}); compute billing periods as half-open intervals [start, end) anchored to a declared timezone; derive next-period boundary as prev.end = next.start with no gap and no overlap. *Falsifier: DST/timezone drift inserts a free day or double-bills a day; gap or overlap between periods bills the customer incorrectly.*

**D2. Proration calculator** — Compute proration_factor = days_remaining / days_in_current_period (billing-timezone denominator supplied by D1); apply to credit (old_plan_price) and charge (new_plan_price); for annual↔monthly direction, use price-per-day = plan_price / days_in_period, not the complementary plan's price. *Falsifier: constant denominator fails on Feb, 31-day months, leap years; wrong rate direction systematically mis-credits the customer.*

**D3. Invoice assembly** — Build invoice as Σ(line_items) + tax − applied_credits; each line item must be attributed to exactly one billing period; seal as an immutable document on finalization. *Falsifier: orphaned (unattributed) line item causes revenue leakage with no audit trail; mutation after finalization breaks the audit trail.*

**D4. Tax computation** — Compute tax_amount = taxable_base × rate(country, plan_type, effective_date) from a versioned rate table; determine taxable_base per country as either tax-inclusive (back-compute net) or tax-exclusive (gross-up). *Falsifier: unversioned rate table applies a new rate to already-finalized invoices; inclusive/exclusive mismatch inflates stated price and reports wrong net revenue.*

**D5. Credit ledger** — Maintain running account_balance = Σcredit_ledger_entries − Σdebit_ledger_entries; write a ledger row on every credit or debit event; apply credits before the charge attempt; enforce balance ≥ 0. *Falsifier: balance updated without a ledger row creates a phantom balance that reconciliation cannot explain; negative balance passes undetected until the gateway rejects a negative-amount charge.*

**D6. Charge & dunning engine** — Issue every charge attempt with an idempotency key; build dunning schedule as an ordered list of retry intervals anchored to the charge-failure timestamp; model grace period as an explicit subscription state; transition subscription to a declared terminal state after max retries. *Falsifier: missing idempotency key causes double-charge on network retry; schedule not anchored to failure time allows indefinitely-timed or post-cancellation retries.*

**D7. Refund processor** — Compute full_refund = total net paid on invoice (gross charges − prior partial refunds on same invoice); partial_refund = explicitly specified amount ≤ net paid (never re-derived from current plan price); issue a credit note for every refund; select refund destination (original payment method vs. account credit) explicitly. *Falsifier: second full refund on a partially-refunded invoice over-refunds; re-deriving refund amount after a plan change gives wrong value; absent destination selection fails silently on an expired payment method.*

**D8. Subscription lifecycle FSM** — States: {trialing, active, past_due, paused, canceled}; all transitions must be declared and exhaustive; the following policies must each be explicit: trial_end anchored to trial_start timestamp (not signup); exactly one trial-converted event at trial end; pause entry = immediate or at period boundary; pause exit triggers billing for remaining days and extends period by pause duration; cancellation effective date = immediate or end-of-period; reactivation anchor = reactivation date; plan-change effective date = immediate (proration applies) or next renewal (no proration). *Falsifier: implicit or missing transition leaves status inconsistent with billing events; wrong anchor date shifts billing.*

## Dep — Cross-Component Dependency Seams

**Dep-1. D2 + D4 → D3 (plan-change × proration × tax)** — Each proration line (credit and charge) carries its own independent tax computation; tax must NOT be computed on the net proration amount. *Breaks: tax on the net amount gives wrong result in jurisdictions with minimum thresholds or tiered rates.*

**Dep-2. D8 → D5 (trial-end × credit balance)** — At trial conversion, any existing account credit must be consumed before the first charge attempt is issued. *Breaks: first charge ignores existing credits; customer charged more than owed.*

**Dep-3. D8 → D2 (trial-end × annual plan)** — The trial-conversion event triggers a full annual charge; no day-offset from the trial period is applied to the annual billing amount. *Breaks: trial-day proration applied to the annual price produces a partial-year first charge.*

**Dep-4. D6 + D8 → D3 (dunning × plan-change [past_due + upgrade])** — An outstanding unpaid invoice must be resolved (paid, voided, or merged) before or alongside any new plan charge when the subscription is in past_due. *Breaks: two concurrent invoices leave dunning with an ambiguous collection target.*

**Dep-5. D6 → D8 (dunning × cancellation)** — Cancellation during active dunning must declare whether the unpaid invoice is voided or remains collectible. *Breaks: implicit void loses revenue; implicit keep blocks reactivation by requiring payment of an aged invoice.*

**Dep-6. D8 + D2 → D7 (cancellation × annual proration)** — Immediate cancellation of an annual plan must apply a declared, consistent policy on whether the remaining period generates a refund. *Breaks: undeclared policy produces different outcomes depending on which code path is reached.*

**Dep-7. D8 → D5 (cancellation × credits)** — Unused account credits at cancellation must be assigned one declared fate: refunded to customer, forfeited, or held for reactivation. *Breaks: no policy causes credits to silently vanish or block account closure.*

**Dep-8. D8 + D6 → D3 (reactivation × dunning debt)** — On reactivation, any unpaid invoice from the prior dunning cycle must be explicitly handled (revived, written off, or merged) before a new invoice is created. *Breaks: new invoice created while old debt is silently written off or double-counted.*

**Dep-9. D8 + D2 → D6 (pause × billing period [resume charge])** — On resume from a mid-period pause, the charge covers only the days remaining at pause, not the full period. *Breaks: full-period charge on resume double-bills the pre-pause days.*

**Dep-10. D8 → D1 (pause × annual renewal date)** — Paused days must shift the annual renewal date by the exact pause duration. *Breaks: renewal fires on the original anniversary, cutting the customer's paid term short.*

**Dep-11. D6 → D8 (pause × dunning [past_due gate])** — A subscription in past_due must not enter pause until payment is resolved, or pausing must continue dunning retries without interruption. *Breaks: pausing a past_due subscription halts retries; unpaid invoice ages silently with no collection action.*

**Dep-12. D7 → D4 (refund × tax)** — A refund on a taxed invoice must include the proportional tax portion, not only the pre-tax amount. *Breaks: partial refund of the pre-tax amount leaves the customer under-refunded by the tax fraction.*

**Dep-13. D7 → D5 (refund → credit balance)** — A refund issued as account credit must write a ledger entry; all subsequent invoices must consume the resulting balance. *Breaks: credit created by the refund not reflected in the ledger causes future charges to ignore it.*

**Dep-14. D5 → D4 (credit balance × tax)** — Where jurisdiction rules require it, if a credit reduces the invoice amount the taxable base must be recomputed on the reduced amount before tax is finalized. *Breaks: tax computed on gross then credit applied — customer pays tax on an amount they were credited back.*

**Dep-15. D5 + D6 → D8 (annual × monthly co-existence [dunning scope])** — Account-level credits apply across all subscriptions; dunning on one subscription must not trigger cancellation of other, healthy subscriptions on the same account. *Breaks: dunning logic scoped to the account cancels a healthy subscription alongside the delinquent one.*

**Dep-16. D6 → D8 (trial-end × gateway-down)** — If the payment gateway is unavailable at trial end, the subscription must be routed into dunning retry logic, not transitioned to canceled. *Breaks: gateway-down at trial end marks the subscription past_due or canceled instead of queuing a retry.*

**Dep-17. D8 → D4 (reactivation × country change)** — Tax country is re-evaluated at reactivation date, not inherited from the canceled subscription record. *Breaks: old tax country applied to the first post-reactivation invoice produces wrong tax.*

**Dep-18. D1 → D2 (proration × leap year)** — D2 must use the actual period length (days_in_current_period) supplied by D1, not a constant 365. *Breaks: annual period containing Feb 29 has 366 days; using 365 is an off-by-one error.*

**Dep-19. D5 → D7 (credit application × partial refund)** — The refundable base on an invoice is invoice_total − applied_credits (net amount charged), not the gross invoice total. *Breaks: refund against gross total refunds more than the customer actually paid.*

**Dep-20. D5 → D2 (multiple plan changes in one period)** — Credits from the first plan change must be reflected in the credit balance before computing the second proration charge. *Breaks: each change computed against the original plan price; accumulated credits ignored; customer over-charged.*

**Dep-21. D6 → D8 (charge in flight × plan change)** — A plan change must not be finalized until any pending charge attempt on the current subscription has settled (success or failure). *Breaks: plan recorded as upgraded before charge result arrives; if charge fails, plan is upgraded with no payment.*

## V — Criteria

*(Each is a decidable predicate over the produced result.)*

**V1. Invoice total invariant + immutability** — invoice_total = Σ(line_items) + tax − applied_credits at finalization; the document is immutable thereafter — no retroactive mutation of any component, including tax recalculation triggered by a mid-period country change.

**V2. Charge idempotency** — Processing the same charge event twice produces exactly one charge record; no duplicate revenue entry and no double-charge to the customer.

**V3. Ledger balance consistency** — account_balance = Σcredit_ledger_entries − Σdebit_ledger_entries at all times; every credit or debit event has a corresponding ledger row.

**V4. No overlapping active periods** — No two billing periods for the same subscription overlap in time.

**V5. Currency consistency** — All line items, tax, and credits on one invoice share the same currency.

**V6. Tax-period consistency** — The tax rate applied to an invoice is the rate effective at invoice issue date, not at period start or payment date.

**V7. Charge attempt ↔ invoice linkage** — Every charge attempt is traceable to exactly one invoice; no orphaned charge attempt exists without a corresponding invoice.

**V8. Refund ≤ net paid** — Total refunds on an invoice cannot exceed total net amount collected (gross charges minus all prior partial refunds on the same invoice).

**V9. Active subscription ↔ open period** — Every subscription in {trialing, active, past_due} has exactly one open billing period; none has zero or more than one.

**V10. Zero-amount invoice** — When applied credits fully cover the invoice, amount_due = 0 and no charge attempt is issued.

**V11. Negative invoice** — When credits exceed the invoice total, the excess is added to account balance and no outbound payment is triggered.

**V12. Same-day plan change** — A plan change on day 1 of the billing period produces: proration credit = 0, new-plan charge = full period (no division-by-zero, no 100% credit on the old plan).

**V13. Zero-day trial** — A zero-length trial produces the same result as an immediate paid subscription; no trial-converted event is emitted.

**V14. Cancellation during trial** — No invoice, no line items, and no refund attempt are present in the output when a subscription is canceled while still in trial.

**V15. Pause on day 1 of period** — After a pause begun on day 1 of a period and subsequent resume, the billing output shows the full period provided with no charge for the paused interval.

**V16. Resume on boundary day of max pause window** — A resume event on the last day of the allowed pause duration is treated as a valid resume; no auto-cancellation is triggered.

**V17. Same-day cancellation and reactivation** — When cancellation and reactivation timestamps fall on the same calendar day, their ordering is determined by timestamp; the subscription is not left in an inconsistent state.

**V18. Feb 29 annual anniversary** — An annual renewal date of Feb 29 is rolled to Feb 28 in the following non-leap year; no date-arithmetic exception occurs.

**V19. Dunning terminal state** — After all dunning retries are exhausted, the subscription is in a declared terminal state; it is not left active with no collection path and dunning does not run indefinitely.

**V20. No charge to a canceled subscription** — No charge record is created for a subscription whose status is canceled, even if a dunning retry job was enqueued before the cancellation.

**V21. Tax completeness at finalization** — Every finalized invoice in a taxable jurisdiction carries a non-null, correctly computed tax_amount; no invoice is finalized while its tax computation is still pending.

**V22. No refund against a pending charge** — No refund record is created against a charge attempt that has not yet reached a terminal gateway state (success or failure).

**V23. Credits not consumed by canceled subscription** — No credit balance decrement is attributed to a subscription with status = canceled; the balance remains available for active subscriptions only.

**V24. Annual→monthly downgrade credit rate** — The proration credit for an annual→monthly downgrade equals annual_price × (days_remaining / days_in_year), not unused_full_months × monthly_price.

**V25. Monthly→annual upgrade invoice completeness** — A monthly→annual upgrade invoice contains both the full annual charge line and the remaining monthly-days credit line; neither is absent.

## N — Declared Scope Exclusions

**N1. Payment gateway communication** — Sending charge requests to the gateway is infrastructure; this scope receives only the outcome (success / failed / pending) as input. *In only if retry timing must be governed by specific gateway response codes — the dunning schedule's input signal already models this boundary.*

**N2. Email / notification dispatch** — Sending dunning emails and billing notices is a side effect of state transitions, not calculation logic. *In only if email-send failure is required to gate a billing state transition.*

**N3. Payment method storage / tokenization** — The card-token vault is infrastructure; calculation logic does not touch it. *In only if payment method type affects tax treatment (e.g., B2B purchase order vs. consumer card).*

**N4. Accounting / GL export** — Posting to the general ledger is downstream of invoice creation; GL consumes finalized invoices. *In only if revenue-recognition rules (ASC 606) determine when a charge line is booked, affecting invoice timing.*

**N5. Product catalog / feature entitlement** — What features a plan grants is out of scope; calculation needs only plan price and billing period. *In only if entitlements affect proration (e.g., seat-based or usage-based billing).*

**N6. Fraud detection** — Detecting abnormal charge patterns is a separate system; billing logic does not gate on fraud scores. *In only if a fraud-flagged charge must be voided before invoice finalization.*

**N7. Multi-currency FX rates** — Out if all plans are denominated in a single currency. *In as soon as a credit earned in one currency must be applied to an invoice in another.*
