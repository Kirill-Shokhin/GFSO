# Why the product is built this way

This page maps what you meet when you use GFSO to the clause of the canon that requires it. [`USING_GFSO.md`](USING_GFSO.md) says how to run it and changes with the code; [`THEORY.md`](THEORY.md) and [the canon](applied_gfso_v4_en.md) say what it stands on and change only with the canon. This page changes when a product decision does. For each behaviour it gives the § that forces it, or, where the canon leaves a freedom, the choice the product made and what that choice trades. The grade words are the canon's own (§0.1): *derived* follows by an argument the canon states, *defined* fixes what a term is, *adopted* is a choice the canon makes, a design decision or a reading it takes.

Two words carry most of what follows. A node's **issuer** is the party that handed it down, the holder of its parent; the issuer forms the node's contract, its **packet** (spec, criteria, deadline), and signs its verdict. Its **executor** does the work and delivers it (§10.1).

## "Done" is the root's PASS, over a check its executor did not make

Only the `PASS` of the plan's top node counts as done, and that `PASS` stands only on a check of the delivery that its executor did not make. Under a mode that check is the system validator's record, or a record made by hand by the node's issuer where the issuer is not its executor; a record by anyone else is kept, marked as made by hand, and counts for nothing. The engine refuses a `PASS` whose record carries a criterion with no value of its own (Inv-2, §12.8). The machine enters DONE only through PASS (§12.4, derived), and acceptance stands only by a PASS on the log (§12.11, adopted).

A node whose executor differs from its parent's is a seam, and there the canon requires separation of duties: the verdict is signed by the issuer, independently, over a check that is not the executor's (§10.3, derived). Below a seam a node may verify itself, because the verdict at the seam above it carries that work (§10.3, derived). A root your own session issues and holds, under `solo` or `semi`, is no seam: its signer is the goal's owner, outside the protocol's incentive surface, and the canon asks no second party there (§16.13, derived). The product puts another party's check under it anyway, as an implementation may (§10.2): its validator observes the root's delivery, and that record binds the signature, so your session's `PASS` is refused until the check passes and refused past a divergence it records under the criteria in force. The canon names what this buys: a second damper and a detection probability for a false `PASS` (§16.13, said there of a check below a seam), not separation of duties. The contract and the signature stay your session's, as the goal's owner; the check never is.

## A part larger than one session is observed by the system

A node whose executor is its parent's is internal, and the canon lets it verify itself: the public verdict above it covers its work (§10.3, derived). The product adds one observation inside that freedom. At the top of every part of the plan that one executor session does not carry whole, the system's validator runs the criteria on the delivery, internal or not. Where the node's issuer is its own executor, a divergence the validator finds binds the signature. The ground is the leaf test: above an agent's capacity κ the executor's own check is no longer a direct check against the world (§3.7, derived). The canon lets an implementation run a check of its own on every delivery (§10.2). The same test makes a node above its holder's κ owe a split, before execution or in performance (§3.7); the product asks for it as soon as the node is judged larger than one session. Inside one session's scope nothing is observed piece by piece, unless `GFSO_VALIDATE_INTERNAL=1` asks for it for measurement.

## solo, semi and auto are one protocol with different holders

The mode says who holds which nodes: your session holds all of them (`solo`), the top of the plan (`semi`), or none (`auto`, where the system holds every node, the root included). The verbs, the checks and the machine are the same in every mode. In the canon, tasks, splits, dependencies and verdicts make no reference to agents, and agency enters only through Del (§2.8, derived), which assigns each task exactly one agent (§2.6, defined; its uniqueness adopted). The protocol and the check battery are identical at every level of autonomy, and what changes is Del (§10.4).

The canon makes autonomy a property of each task and role, "not a global mode" (§10.4, defined). A mode set per project is the product's choice against that grain. It gives up choosing a holder node by node, keeping three patterns and `semi`'s depth boundary, in exchange for one decision a person can make before the plan exists. Once a project has work in it, its mode is locked, and only you change it, from the project's page: changing Del changes who is accountable, and an agent's connection, the CLI and a named token are refused, since an agent that types the person's words could otherwise take the person's nodes.

Your agent names `auto` as the default and goes on, saying so. A default holder said to you is the counterpart of a default deadline the canon admits "logged as supplied" (§2.5, adopted): the protocol is the same at every level, and the switch is Del (§10.4), which you change by asking for `solo` or `semi`. Nothing becomes an autopilot that you were not told about.

Under `semi`, a node your session holds above the boundary is not taken or handed in until it is split, because below the boundary the system executes. The refusal names that requirement, and you lift it yourself with a truthful move the canon admits from the offer: a split, even one of a single child (§11.5, §12.16 (ii)). Work your session wants to do whole is `solo`.

## perform: dispatch or in_chat

A node your session holds is performed in one of two ways. Under `dispatch`, the default, your session consents and stays the node's executor, while a clean executor session of the project does the work in the node's own place. Under `in_chat`, your session does the work with its own tools in your repository, and its delivery is the commit it stands on at DELIVER. Either way the node's Del is your session, and the seam and its verdict do not change. `perform` changes who carries out the work, not who answers for it. The canon requires exactly one accountable party per task (§2.6, adopted). It separates the check from the signature, which is the issuer's (§10.2), and derives seams from Del alone (§10.3). Under `in_chat` nothing but your session keeps the work inside the contract, so the agent instructions ask you before choosing it.

## Your name signs from your page or from your own agent

You and your own agent share one name, `user`: your agent acts for you. A move signed as `user` comes from one of two places: your page, or an MCP connection of your agent's that has taken no stream's name, which is a bridge carrying its lease or a stdio server a client launched. A process of yours that carries no credential, no bridge and no page's headers is not taken for your agent and only reads. That covers a script, `curl`, and `gfso run` from any shell, whether a server is up or not. Its move is refused, and the refusal names three ways to sign: your page, your agent's gfso connection, or, for a stream, a token from `gfso agent <name>`. A connection that took a stream's name signs as that name alone, never as `user`. The signature is the issuer's (§10.2), and Del names the holder of the role (§10.1). The name a move carries is therefore a claim about who holds the role, and a console any process can open would make that claim for you. The CLI gets the same rule with or without a server: either way it is a shell any agent's tool can run, and the door does not change who is at it. This keeps an agent's casual call off your name, and it is no barrier. Locally your OS user is still the boundary: any process of yours can open a bridge, or send a browser's headers and be your page by default, and [`SECURITY.md`](../SECURITY.md) says so. `--protected` guards your page by its sign-in, and a console takes a stream's name only with that name's token. Your agent's bridge and stdio server are your agent there too: managing your agents is what they are for.

## A seam you issue keeps your word by default

In a project with no mode, a node you issue to another name is a seam whose issuer is you (§10.1, §10.3). Every issuer move on it is yours: its verdict, its packet, its answers, its re-issue (§10.2). Your page makes such a move as it is. An agent's connection that signs as your name makes it only with your words, passed as `user_said` and recorded on the feed. Otherwise the agent working beside you would sign your role. Below the seam, every node is issued by its holder, so none of this applies there. A call that no door served is the system's own and keeps the earlier rule. Under a mode, the mode's record answers who holds each node, and your word stands at your root under `solo` and `semi`.

When you issue such a seam, its packet carries the criterion `answers_the_persons_words`, read against what you said over it and over the nodes above it. Your act installs it at the seam's first ASSIGN, the counterpart of a go on your own root (the author, 2026-11-19: on by default). A later move of the system never installs it, and the switch on your page turns it off.

## A seam's holder working in its chat holds it solo, by its own act

Autonomy is a property of each task and role (§10.4, defined). When a stream's name moves over its own MCP connection, taken there with `act_as` or launched with its token, that records that the name performs in a chat. It is the executor's own fact, and nobody sets it for it. From then on, every seam you issue to that name is held as your own solo agent holds your project: its holder works and splits the whole of it in its chat, the seam's contract and verdict stay yours, and nothing switches your branch, since the checkout is shared. No button sets this, and the earlier `set_mode(node=…)` is refused with that reason. A project mode, where one is set, answers alone. A scope recorded by an earlier build still reads its seams' holders as working in a chat.

## A seam you issue is checked by the validator before it reaches you

At every seam you issue, the project's validator checks each delivery against every criterion, `answers_the_persons_words` included, before the result is brought to you. Nobody turns it off there. You may ask for it again, but `set_checker(…, "self")` is refused. This is your word (2026-11-20): «по gfso на шве (то есть перед тем как принести результат мне) должен стоять обязательный. который проверит на соответствие исходному замыслу и соответствие всем явным критериям и не даст сказать готово … и мне не придется держать в памяти критерии неедльной давности … и система заставит уходить на реворк после непрохождения шва». The canon lets an implementation run a check of its own on every delivery (§10.2). The check comes before the result reaches you, so it gates the holder's delivery. The holder's DELIVER of a tree is refused while the validator's latest check of that tree failed, or no check of it exists, and the refusal names the failed criteria. The holder reworks, commits, and has the tree checked again: `validate_result` on the node at work judges its committed tree and moves nothing. It delivers once the check passes. §12.16 (ii) admits that refusal because a standing verdict under the current criteria indicts the tree, and the lift is truthful work. The signature stays yours, and the system makes none of your moves. Once the tree is delivered your PASS stands on that check. While the check is owed, or a FAIL of it stands, your PASS is refused, and a record by your own hand does not stand in for it. A delivery that records no tree, or one a registered machine makes, is checked after it lands, under the same rule for your PASS. Where the project has no validator registered, your issue of the seam and its delivery name the registration.

## The plan is a file, read whole before the go

The system writes the plan as a file you can read in an editor ([`PLAN_FORMAT.md`](PLAN_FORMAT.md)). Your agent, as its customer, reads it whole and either sends a note or gives the go. It does not wait for you unless you ask to read the plan first. The go either builds the project from the file exactly, with every criterion and its pinned check, or is refused with the reason. Under `auto` the agent door will not make a new root from a bare request, so every go is on a plan someone has read. After the go, a criterion changes only by a logged revision. The canon lets a split be drafted and the battery run over the draft before issue (§11.5). It makes the packet immutable after ASSIGN, the act that hands a node over: any change is a re-ASSIGN that the executor consents to anew (§12.3, Inv-1, derived from FM-5). Pre-registration rests on that rule (§12.14).

## A split can wait until contact makes it definable

A part of the plan may carry `split_after`. Its split is then made only after named producers' results are accepted, with their verdicts and files in hand. The build refuses a deferral that names no such producer or gives no reason. FM-6 is a split fixed before the information that decides it could be known, and its remedy is to defer the split until contact makes it definable (§8.6). The canon also says that a node's split need not exist at its ASSIGN: the node's executor may write it while performing the node (§11.5).

## The validator is an instrument; the verdict is the issuer's

Every mode needs a validator registered for the project. On each delivery it runs the criteria and reports on each one. Where the node's issuer is the system, that report becomes the issuer's verdict. Where the issuer is a person, the validator runs and records its report, and the node waits until that person signs. A report that decides nothing is never read as a pass, and one that cannot be read is never signalled as a verdict: until checking completes, a verdict is absent, not a third value (§2.3, defined), and an attempt that yields none is no event of the machine (§12.2, defined). The canon makes validation two acts. The check evaluates the criteria on the result as delivered; at an internal node whoever performs it may run it, at a seam it must not be the executor's (§10.3). The signature belongs to the node's issuer (§10.2).

## What is validated is the delivered commit

Where DELIVER recorded a commit, a validation runs in a checkout of that commit and nothing else: no ignored files, no built binaries, nothing changed in the executor's place after DELIVER, and the system runs the build DELIVER declares, or a root `Makefile`, before any check. A delivery with no commit recorded, such as one from a directory that is not a git repository, is copied as it stands, as [`SECURITY.md`](../SECURITY.md) says. Work an executor continues after delivering reaches a verdict only through a new delivery. In the canon, a delivery presents the result as it stands at DELIVER (§11.3). The verdict is a value of the criteria over the result as delivered, true afterwards exactly when it was true at delivery (§8.6, the after phase, derived).

## The plan's form is checked at three levels: the first gates admission, the review gates execution

The canon reads a split at three levels (§9.1). The Syntactic level, Level 0, reads the graph alone, and the product gates admission on it: a split with an uncovered criterion, an orphan child, a cycle, incoherent deadlines or a missing risk register is refused (§9.5, run at admission by §11.5). The Semantic level, Level 1, reads what the criteria formally entail and whether they can all hold together. The product runs its numeric part on every split and reports, without gating. The canon says such cheap checks always run (§9.9). It asks the decomposer to discharge a Semantic flag before admission and names what a split admitted with one undischarged carries (§9.7, §11.5); the product leaves that discharge to the decomposer, with the flag in front of him. The Pragmatic level, Level 2, asks whether the children's criteria, taken as facts about the world, carry the parent's. The canon says only execution checks it (§9.1). Before execution the product runs a model review in its place, `review_decomposition`, which also covers the semantic part no formula reaches, and by default no child starts until its parent's split has a current review with nothing left open. A child its parent's own executor holds, under a parent one session carries whole, is the exception: once the parent's Syntactic level is clean it starts unreviewed, and contact checks it (§9.9, EXPLORE). You fix each of its findings or dispute it in writing, and contact keeps the last word. A plan file the system wrote carries its own review, and its go turns the gate off under that plan alone, never for the project's other goals; a file its customer wrote carries none, and its split is reviewed as any other. The canon derives the choice between paying for a check in advance and letting contact check it (§9.9, VERIFY vs EXPLORE) and fixes no costs, so the product makes skipping the review a setting (`l2_gate=False`, `GFSO_L2_GATE=0`). A skipped step cannot turn the review off, and with no checker available the gate stays shut.

## Every wait has a bound

Each node carries a deadline. If you give none, the engine writes a default inside the parent's deadline and records that it supplied it. Each waiting state also has its own bound. By default that is a day for an answer owed or for the issuer's decision, and an hour for a verdict, counted from the delivery. The deadline runs through every state of the contract, BLOCKED and CHALLENGED included. The canon says that activity without a time bound cannot be told apart from inactivity, and it allows a default deadline that is logged as supplied (§2.5, adopted). Every wait has a bound, and the packet deadline is never suspended (§12.2, defined). Every non-terminal state except IDLE times out (§12.12, Inv-5). The canon fixes no values for the bounds (§12.2), so the numbers are the product's. Setting the server's `GFSO_BOUND_<STATE>` to `0` removes that bound, and that departure from the canon is yours; a project's own bounds refuse `0`.

One departure is the product's own, and only where the system's own roles carry the work. There a project's time counts only while the server runs it and the project is not stopped: a host asleep, a server down or a stop keeps that time off every clock in the project. Without it, a run stopped overnight would wake with every node in work timed out by a wait no one was running. A graph people hold keeps the canon's clock, the wall: nothing of its time is left off, and a stop there holds no deadline back, so one that passes during the stop times out then. The system stops a project itself only where its own roles carry the work. An account at its limit or an execution place down lifts by itself once the cause is gone. The stop a server's start makes lifts when you start the project again, as your own stop does. When a stop that held time lifts, or the project runs again after a server down or a host asleep, every live deadline reads that much later, and each node records the move: the date before and after, how long the time held, and who or what stopped it and started it again. The canon never suspends a deadline (§12.2), so that record is the departure's own: every decision has a record (Thm 11). It is no revision, so the node keeps its contract and the consent its executor gave to it. Each held second is left off once and recorded once, however many processes open the project — a server and a stdio MCP beside it, or a CLI verb. The few seconds of an ordinary restart, under two minutes, are no gap: the time runs on and nothing moves.

## A lapse hands the decision to the issuer

When a wait runs out or the rework budget is spent, the node goes to ESCALATED and the decision passes to its issuer. The issuer either continues, raising the bound or changing the criteria as a revision, or closes with `CANCEL`. Silence closes the node by timeout. A VALIDATING that runs out goes back to the issuer without a verdict. Under `auto` the system is the issuer: it makes the same moves, or splits the node above again, and at the root it acts for the goal's customer. The canon says that ESCALATED settles nothing, that the clock never invents a verdict, and that silence takes the closing path (§12.5, defined). Every state in which work has begun has an owner with a move of its own (§12.13, Inv-8), so under `auto` the system holds the issuer's moves.

## A refusal names what it requires and how to lift it

A refused go names why. A refused deferral of a split names its two lifts: name the producer, or split the part now. A refused split lists its holes. The canon's conformance clause allows an implementation to refuse a move the canon admits only if the refusal names the requirement it enforces, and only where the sender can lift it in time by truthful moves that keep the work (§12.16 (ii), defined). A refusal that could be lifted only by misreporting or by discarding work would make honesty non-conforming (§12.16, derived).

## A changed contract is a revision of the same node

Changing a node's criteria, deadline or budget keeps its id and its subtree, whether through `revise`, `edit_criteria` or an accepted `CHALLENGE`. The executor then consents again. A task's id is stable over its whole life, one node carrying the successive versions of its spec, with past versions in the log (§12.11, Inv-7, defined); so a change is in-place re-authorship, never delete and create (§12.11, derived). Changing any field of the packet is a re-ASSIGN under the same id, and it binds the executor only through a new ACCEPT (§12.3, Inv-1, derived).

## A closed node is reopened only while nothing rests on it

`reopen` returns a settled node to be earned again. It works only while nothing rests on the node and within its reopen budget; after that, recovery means splitting again around the node. Under `auto`, where the issuer is the system, your agent's door refuses it. The canon's base machine makes a settlement irreversible (§12.14, adopted). The product runs the canon's extension R′, where a reopen is the issuer's re-ASSIGN under a per-node counter, admissible only while the settlement is not final in the canon's sense (§12.14). The node goes back to OFFERED, and its PASS is removed, not carried forward.

## Dispatched work runs in Docker, and your branches are never written

This is a product promise, not a canon clause. Work dispatched to the system's executors, and its checks, run in containers, and nothing of your disk is mounted into them. Your repository enters as its committed HEAD, with all its history, which every session can read: clean a secret out of your history before the first run. The result comes back as the branch `gfso/<project>/accepted`, and your working tree and branches stay untouched. Your own agent under `perform="in_chat"` and a host executor you allow on your page work on your machine; [`SECURITY.md`](../SECURITY.md) names everything that runs there and what crosses each boundary. The canon does not say where work runs. The reason for this choice is practical: agents run unattended, with no one to approve a command.
